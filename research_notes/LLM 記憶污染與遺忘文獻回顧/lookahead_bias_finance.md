## Key Question 1 — Definitions and conceptual framing (and corrected citations)

### Takeaway
The literature converges on one definition: an LLM exhibits look-ahead bias when its *parameters* (not only its inputs) encode information that post-dates the moment the prediction is supposed to be made, so that "historical" predictions violate the orthogonality between a time-t forecast and time-t+1 surprises. Three framings matter for a fraud-scoring project: (i) Sarkar & Vafa's pretraining-leakage definition, (ii) Ludwig–Mullainathan–Rambachan's formal "no training leakage" condition, and (iii) Lopez-Lira–Tang–Zhu's non-identification result (for any pre-cutoff period, skill and memorization are observationally equivalent, and memorization tests give only a lower bound).

**B. Definitions**

- Sarkar & Vafa: a model's pretraining data can contain information about the future, which then leaks into analyses meant to use only past information; their tests rest on the assumption that some events cannot be predicted from a given information set. They find the bias in two applications (risk factors from earnings calls; election winners from candidate biographies), discuss the limits of prompting-based fixes, and recommend models whose pretraining data is free of survivorship bias and contains only language produced before the analysis period. [V-snip] — [ICML 2025 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/48685); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678)
- Gao, Jiang & Yan formalize Sarkar & Vafa's definition as a violation of orthogonality, Cov(μ̂_t, ε_{t+1}) ≠ 0, and model the LLM forecast as μ̂_t = μ(X_t) + γ·L_t·ε_{t+1} (γ = model-level "contamination loading", L_t = firm-period memorization strength). — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847)
- Glasserman & Lin distinguish two channels by which model knowledge contaminates text-based signals: **look-ahead bias** (the LLM "may have specific knowledge of the stock returns that followed a news article"; expected positive, in-sample only) and the **distraction effect** ("general knowledge of the companies named interferes with the measurement of a text's sentiment"; found to be negative in-sample). — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1)
- Ludwig, Mullainathan & Rambachan: valid prediction conclusions "require 'no training leakage' between the LLM's training data and the researcher's sample"; formally (Proposition 1) a leakage term — which weights the loss by how much being sampled by the researcher updates beliefs about inclusion in training — must be zero; they liken it to omitted-variable bias. They stress that verbatim reproduction is "not a necessary condition for training leakage". For *estimation* (as opposed to prediction) uses of LLM outputs they recommend small validation samples to correct measurement error. — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)
- Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu: "Counterfactual forecasting ability is non-identified when the model has seen the realized values." They introduce *functional-form look-ahead bias* (post-cutoff information embedded in parameters contaminates the decision rule even when inputs are clean), a distinction between **future-variant** tasks (sentiment classification, risk extraction, forecasting) and **future-invariant** tasks (e.g., factual extraction), and Remark 1: memorization tests give only a lower bound — positive evidence is conclusive, negative evidence is not. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- He, Lv, Manela & Wu define look-ahead bias / training leakage as models learning from "data that did not exist at the historical moment". — [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3)
- Wu, Yang, Ying & Zhou: bias arises when LLMs use "information that would not have been accessible to an agent prior to the model's knowledge cutoff". — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15364v1)
- Didisheim, Fraschini & Somoza: look-ahead bias occurs when an LLM is trained on information that would not have been available when its predictions were made, inflating apparent performance; the bias depends predictably on data frequency, model size and aggregation level. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v256y2025ics0165176525004392.html)
- Levy (JAR 2026 abstract): much of AI models' superior performance in accounting/finance tasks comes from artifacts of the modeling process — (1) very poor numerical reasoning and (2) significant look-ahead bias in commercial LLMs, which may account for a large share of their predictive ability. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v64y2026i3p1139-1188.html)
- Kong et al. (2026 position paper): "Look-ahead bias arises when a backtest uses information that did not exist at historical time" t; two channels — parametric knowledge in weights and external context via retrieval (RAG). Later 2026 papers adopt the label **"parametric look-ahead bias"** for the weights channel (FinCAD; FinCutBench). — [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233); [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564); [arXiv 2611.11588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11588)
- Zhang & Stadie (2026) add a third confound to the look-ahead/skill dichotomy: **recency** — a model legitimately knows more about periods close to its cutoff, so "recency mimics leakage". — [arXiv 2608.02985](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02985)
- Crane, Karra & Soto document a macro-data variant: LLMs blend first-print data with later revisions and mix reference periods, and "aggregated across series" tend to believe they hold data that had not yet been released on a given day — limiting their use to mimic real-time forecasters. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedgfe/2025-44.html)

### Inferences
- For a fraud-risk score produced from a pre-scandal 10-K, the task is "future-variant" in Lopez-Lira et al.'s sense (the counterfactual model that never saw the collapse would likely answer differently), so the non-identification result applies directly: a high score for Enron FY2000 cannot by itself be attributed to reading the filing.
- The Glasserman–Lin "distraction effect" implies the contamination is not only upward: knowledge of a firm's later fame/infamy can distort the reading of the text in either direction, so naive "the bias only helps us" reasoning is not safe.
- The field's vocabulary shifted between 2024 and 2026 from "look-ahead bias" (finance) / "training leakage" (econometrics) / "temporal leakage" (ML forecasting) to "parametric look-ahead bias" or "parametric hindsight"; a literature review should treat these as the same construct.

## Key Question 2 — How do these papers PROVE look-ahead bias, and how large is it?

### Takeaway
Six families of tests are used: (1) direct recall probes (ask for prices, macro values, dates, firm identity), (2) "impossible knowledge" tests (model mentions things that did not yet exist), (3) pre- vs post-cutoff performance discontinuities, (4) entity-masking A/B comparisons, (5) memorization-propensity interaction regressions, and (6) vintage/placebo comparisons with point-in-time models. Magnitudes range from near-total (GPT-4o recalls S&P 500 levels with 0.61% MAPE pre-cutoff vs 16.87% post-cutoff; identifies Apple/Meta/Microsoft earnings calls 100% of the time after masking) to modest (He et al. find real-time ChronoGPT matches Llama 3.1's Sharpe ratio in news-based return prediction) — the bias is strongly model-, firm-size-, text-length- and task-dependent, and is worst for large, famous firms.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Direct recall probes**

- Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu (GPT-4o, gpt-4o-2024-08-06, cutoff Oct 2023, temperature 0). Asked for daily index levels with no context: pre-cutoff (Jan 1990–Sep 2023) MAPE S&P 500 0.61%, DJIA 0.53%, Nasdaq 1.80%, directional accuracy 80.58% / 80.66% / 69.38%; post-cutoff (Oct 2023–Feb 2025) MAPE 16.87% / 13.01% / 20.48%, directional accuracy 45.70% / 49.26% / 44.03%. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Same paper, macro recall pre-cutoff: threshold accuracy GDP growth 96.27%, inflation 98.02%, unemployment 99.26%, 10-year Treasury 98.52%; post-cutoff GDP 40.00%, unemployment 52.94%, 10-year 29.41%. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Same paper, dating WSJ front-page headlines (90,123 headlines, 1990–Feb 2025): year accuracy 98.45% pre- vs 28.81% post-cutoff; exact-date accuracy 47.03% vs 7.86%; mean absolute error 9.52 days vs 414.54 days. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Same paper, individual stocks (CRSP, >4,200 tickers): no-context pre-cutoff MAPE falls with size from 24.70% (smallest quintile) to 11.29% (largest); with two prior months of prices as context, directional accuracy is ≥74% in all quintiles pre-cutoff. Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct shows the same pattern (macro threshold accuracy >91% pre-cutoff, 60–75% post). — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Didisheim, Fraschini & Somoza prompt LLMs to recall historical stock returns without context and use recall accuracy as a proxy for memorization-driven predictability; bias varies with data frequency, model size and aggregation level, and is negligible for the smallest models and finest granularity. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v256y2025ics0165176525004392.html)
- Crane, Karra & Soto test recall of historical macro variables and release dates: precise for some recent statistics, degrading further back; errors come from smoothing across vintages and across reference periods; release-date knowledge is individually good but jointly leaks unreleased data. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedgfe/2025-44.html). Secondary summaries add that the model was Claude 3.5 Sonnet queried 10 times per item and that LLMs "do not fully follow instructions to ignore future data" [V-snip, unverified against the PDF] — [FEDS PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2025044pap.pdf)
- Ludwig, Mullainathan & Rambachan (GPT-4o): of 10,000 congressional bills (1973–2016) the model predicted House passage with 91.2% accuracy and Senate passage with 92.5% although only 7.4% and 6.0% passed, and reproduced 344 bill descriptions exactly from half the text; from 10,000 financial headlines from 2019 it reproduced 60 exactly. — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)
- Gao, Jiang & Yan's "date-only recall query" (firm name + ticker + date, no text): the direction recalled by Llama-3.3-70B predicts actual next-day returns (pooled θ̂ = 0.264, t = 3.53; top-LAP half 0.306, t = 3.59; bottom half −0.159, t = −0.33), and the recall propensity collapses to ~0 after the Dec 2023 cutoff. — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847)
- FinCutBench (9,000 bilingual MCQs dated Jan 2023–Sep 2026) estimates a per-model "FinCut Boundary" where parametric financial knowledge ends, and reports significant relative declines in U.S. asset-ranking performance and earnings recall beyond it. (Model names in the abstract as fetched — "Sol" and "Luna" — could not be verified.) — [arXiv 2611.11588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11588)

**(3) Pre- vs post-cutoff discontinuities**

- Glasserman & Lin (GPT-3.5-Turbo, training through Sep 2021; in-sample Jan 2015–Sep 2021, out-of-sample Oct 2021–Dec 2022; 129,431 scraped headlines / 6,723 firms and 181,908 Thomson Reuters headlines / 678 S&P 500 firms). Long-short mean daily returns (bp), original vs anonymized: in-sample scraped 25.08 vs 30.97 (t = −1.84, p = 0.066), in-sample TR 10.74 vs 13.84 (t = −2.38, p = 0.017); out-of-sample scraped 16.32 vs 11.09 (t = 1.20), TR 6.07 vs 12.23 (t = −1.86, p = 0.064). — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1)
- Look-Ahead-Bench (agentic trading on AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, NVDA, TSLA; P1 = Apr–Sep 2021 in-sample, P2 = Jul–Dec 2024 out-of-sample): alpha decay Llama 3.1 8B −17.23 pp (+13.81 → −3.42), Llama 3.1 70B −15.25 pp, DeepSeek 3.2 −21.77 pp; point-in-time Pitinf-Small/Medium/Large +0.31 / +0.85 / +1.30 pp. — [arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13770)
- Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev's own check: GPT-4-Turbo (window ending Apr 2023) predicting fiscal-2023 earnings direction from fiscal-2022 statements achieves 58.96% accuracy vs 60.35% in the main (pre-cutoff) sample and vs 59.10% for the ANN benchmark. — [arXiv 2407.17866v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17866v2)
- Levy (JAR 2026), snippet-level: classifiers on representations from LLMs with knowledge cutoffs placed at [t−3, t+3] relative to the firm-year show that models trained on data after the earnings announcement gain "roughly 5%" absolute accuracy relative to models without look-ahead bias; a look-ahead-free GBDT benchmark gives a 2.7% absolute accuracy lift over the best commercial LLM; statements are perturbed by randomizing the least significant digit of each line item to test whether predictions rely on recall. [V-snip from Wiley page search excerpts — re-verify] — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1475-679x.70058)
- Zhang & Stadie caution that the naive pre/post check is **uninformative**: four of five clean flagship models are "flagged" with naive gaps of +0.044 to +0.061 that reflect recency, not leakage; they prove no passive backtest can separate leakage from skill from scores alone. — [arXiv 2608.02985](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02985)

**(4) Masking A/B and re-identification tests**

- Lopez-Lira et al. on entity-neutered earnings calls (pre-cutoff, 2006–Sep 2023): GPT-4o names the firm in 100% of AAPL, META and MSFT calls, 93.55% NVDA, 91.89% GOOGL, 85.71% TSLA, 85.33% AMZN; year accuracy 95.95% for AAPL down to 30.67% for AMZN; by market-cap tercile firm accuracy is 65.77% (large), 40.78% (medium), 30.90% (small). Single masked headlines are much harder (top-decile firm accuracy 7.53% vs 1.41% random), and post-cutoff identification is at chance. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Wu, Yang, Ying & Zhou: GPT-4o-mini re-identifies firms in anonymized earnings-call transcripts at 82.19% (spaCy small NER), 57.42% (spaCy transformer NER) and 19.32% (LLM-based neutering); firm-and-year jointly 36.82% / 8.64% / 4.21%. — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15364v1)
- Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev: on 10,000 anonymized, standardized numeric statements, GPT guessed the firm 0.07% of the time and the fiscal year 2.95% (guesses clustered on 2019–2021 and on Tesla/Facebook/Amazon); accuracy also drops in 1974, 2008–09 and 2020, which the authors read as evidence against memorization. — [arXiv 2407.17866v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17866v2)
- Ludwig et al.: adding date restrictions *and* entity masking to the headline-completion test raised exact reproductions from 60 to 73 — "appears to make the problem worse". — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)

**(5) Memorization-propensity regressions**

- Gao, Jiang & Yan: Lookahead Propensity (LAP = P_up + P_down from the first-token probabilities of a date-only recall query; Llama-3.3-70B). News → next-day returns (Bloomberg, Jan 2012–Dec 2023, 91,357 headlines, 1,587 firms): interaction LLM×LAP δ̂ = 0.162 (t = 3.64); marginal effect of the LLM signal is 0.303% at LAP = 1 vs 0.141% at LAP = 0; a one-SD increase in LAP raises the effect by 0.067 pp (~32% of the standalone effect). Earnings calls → capex (2006Q1–2020Q4, 106,994 firm-quarters): δ̂ = 0.512 (t = 2.01), ~12% amplification per SD. Post-cutoff placebo (2024): interaction insignificant (t = 1.06; t = 1.35 for capex). The test identifies the sign of contamination, not its magnitude. — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847)
- Version note: v1 ("A Test of Lookahead Bias…") is described in secondary summaries as using Min-K% Prob membership inference; v2 defines LAP from date-only recall and does not use Min-K% Prob. — [arXiv 2512.23847 v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847); [v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v1)
- MemGuard-Alpha (negative result): across seven LLMs (124M–7B), 50 S&P 100 firms and 42,800 prompts, raw membership-inference scores look informative (AUC up to 0.99) only because of cross-model scale differences; under within-model normalization discrimination falls to 0.487–0.537, and no contamination filter beats the unfiltered ensemble after costs. — [arXiv 2603.26797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797)

**(6) Point-in-time model comparisons (direct estimates of the bias)**

- He, Lv, Manela & Wu: on Dow Jones Newswire headlines (test Jan 2008–Jul 2023), long-short Sharpe ratios are ChronoGPT-realtime 4.92, Llama 3.1 4.90, ChronoBERT-realtime 4.80, BERT 4.18, FinBERT 3.86, StoriesLM 3.42; the difference between Chrono models and Llama 3.1 is insignificant (p = 0.315, 0.547). Conclusion: look-ahead bias is "modest" in this application and is model- and application-specific. — [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3)
- He et al. (instruction-tuned, prompt-based trading, Jan 2007–Jul 2023): ChronoGPT-Instruct-realtime Sharpe 0.95 vs Qwen-1.5-1.8B-Chat 1.53 and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct 1.76, i.e. "at least 54% to 62%" of apparent predictability survives without leakage; the remainder mixes capacity differences and look-ahead bias. — [arXiv 2510.11677](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11677)
- DatedGPT: search-indexed abstract reports an annualised Sharpe of 3.20 for the look-ahead-free instruct model on ~61,000 firm-day headlines and a "lookahead premium" of 26.4 bp per standard deviation; **these numbers are not in the v1 full text** read in this session (probably a later version) — re-verify. — [alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.11838); [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11838v1)
- Engelberg et al.: the gap between raw-text and neutered-text sentiment predictability is an **upper bound** on look-ahead bias; raw and neutered sentiment agree >95% of the time for unidentified articles with similar return predictability; bounds are larger in high-VIX periods, for stronger-sentiment texts and for **longer texts such as MD&As**. (A later version reports one million articles and non-recognition of firm or period for ~90%.) [V-snip] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5182756)
- FinCAD: removing parametric memory at decoding time cuts in-sample (2010–2020) backtest returns of Qwen2.5-14B by −45.4% on mega-caps (NVDA −78.2%, MSFT −60.8%), −26.7% on mid-caps and −0.9% on small-caps; the largest model-level mean correction is −67.1%; 2025 out-of-sample results are essentially unchanged. — [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564)
- Wu et al. find the pre-cutoff interaction in their sentiment regressions is *negative* (−0.584, t = −3.327 raw), which they read as look-ahead bias playing a limited role in earnings-call sentiment extraction. — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15364v1)
- HindsightBench (15 models, 7 vendors, 258-node macro panel): a "date-trigger reflex" is absent in all measurable 2024 open-weight models but present in every tested 2026-generation model; effective knowledge cutoffs differ by up to 22 months across vendors and can be up to eight months earlier than vendor-reported dates. — [arXiv 2607.18867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867)
- Cheng et al. (Dated Data): effective cutoffs, estimated as the perplexity minimum over dated versions of a resource, often differ from the developer-reported cutoff. — [arXiv 2403.12958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12958)

### Inferences
- Bias magnitude scales with (a) firm prominence (Mag-7: 100% re-identification; large-cap tercile 65.77% vs small 30.90%; FinCAD −45.4% mega vs −0.9% small), (b) text length/richness (earnings calls and MD&As vs single headlines), (c) model size/recency, and (d) outcome salience. Enron, WorldCom and Lehman sit at the extreme on (a), (b) and (d); a full 10-K is the longest input class studied, and the collapses are among the most-written-about corporate events in any training corpus. The "modest bias" finding of He et al. (daily headline sentiment over thousands of firms) should **not** be transferred to this setting.
- Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev's low re-identification rates (0.07% firm) apply to *numbers-only standardized statements*; they explicitly exclude narrative text because it is harder to anonymize (their fn. 30). A 10-K-reading fraud agent uses precisely the narrative text they avoided.
- No paper found measures recall of *scandal outcomes* (fraud, restatement, bankruptcy labels) by date/firm. The recall-probe designs above (date-only query; "what happened to firm X after year Y") are directly portable and would be a novel contribution.
- Because all current frontier models have cutoffs two decades after Enron/WorldCom, the pre/post-cutoff design is unavailable for those cases; only recall probes, masking A/B, memorization-interaction regressions and point-in-time models are feasible.

### Gaps
- The exact Covid-mention rate in Sarkar & Vafa (6.8% vs "over 25%") is unresolved; the primary PDF was unreachable.
- Engelberg et al.'s numerical upper bound on look-ahead bias (in return-predictability units), and their MD&A/earnings-call results, could not be extracted.
- Levy's and Didisheim et al.'s detailed tables (which models, which magnitudes) were not accessible; Levy's numbers above are snippet-level.
- The "debate" on whether GPT recognizes Kim et al.'s anonymized firm-years: no dedicated replication/critique paper was found; the challenge comes indirectly from Levy (numerical reasoning + look-ahead artifacts) and Lopez-Lira et al. (reconstruction from context). Whether Levy directly re-tests the Kim et al. design could not be confirmed from accessible text.

## Key Question 3 — Remedies, with evidence of whether they work

### Takeaway
(1) Anonymization/entity masking reduces but does not eliminate leakage, costs information, and fails most for large famous firms and long texts; (2) point-in-time (chronologically consistent) models are the only remedy that removes the bias *by construction*, at the price of much weaker models and short context windows; (3) strict post-cutoff / live evaluation is the gold standard for *measuring* skill but is unavailable for historical cases and has its own pitfalls (recency, uncertain cutoffs, low power); (4) "pretend you don't know" prompts demonstrably fail in finance tests (though they partly work for directly asked facts); (5) inference-time unlearning (logit adjustment, context-aware decoding, feature steering) is the 2025–26 frontier — promising, but without a ground-truth guarantee.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Entity anonymization / masking**

- Glasserman & Lin replace company names (fuzzy matching + Google Knowledge Graph aliases/products) with a placeholder ("Blahblahblah"); 100,401 of 129,431 scraped and 180,237 of 181,908 TR headlines are modified. Anonymized headlines *outperform* in-sample, i.e. the distraction effect dominates look-ahead bias; the effect is strongest for the larger (TR / S&P 500) firms. Limitations stated: one model (GPT-3.5), ~15-month out-of-sample window, anonymization may not remove all identifying information. — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1)
- Engelberg, Manela, Mullins & Vulicevic ("Entity Neutering"): LLM agents iteratively mask entity-related terms and paraphrase until an independent LLM fails to identify the firm; on >500,000 financial news articles, ChatGPT and other LLMs then identify the subject firm at chance rates; raw/neutered sentiment agree >95% of the time among unidentified articles; also demonstrated on MD&As and earnings-call transcripts, where bounds are larger. [V-snip] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5182756); [talk abstract](https://negocios.uchile.cl/events/seminario-academico-entity-neutering/)
- Counter-evidence — re-identification: Lopez-Lira et al. show GPT-4o identifies the firm in neutered earnings calls for 100% of AAPL/META/MSFT and 65.77% of large-cap calls, and recovers quarter and year (e.g., AAPL year 95.95%); they conclude masking is valid only if the task is future-invariant after masking and skill is still detectable (their Definition 2). — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Lopez-Lira et al.'s stronger alternative — rewriting text into **anonymized economic logic** — cuts identification (NVDA 0.6%, GOOG 3.3%, META 11.9%, but still AAPL 46.3%, TSLA 27.5%; year 3.5–12.2%; exact date 0.0–0.4%) while retaining pre-cutoff directional accuracy of 50.74%–58.19% for the Magnificent Seven. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Counter-evidence — information loss: Wu, Yang, Ying & Zhou (7,382 earnings-call transcripts, 1,831 firms, Nov 2023–Dec 2024, i.e. post-cutoff for GPT-4o-mini/GPT-4o/o3-mini) find information loss from anonymization "more pervasive and severe" than look-ahead bias: in a horse race the anonymized-sentiment coefficient falls from 2.331 to 0.775 (a 67% loss) while raw sentiment stays large and significant; adjusted R² without controls drops from 0.078 to 0.070; with GPT-4o the coefficient drops from 2.528 to 2.005 (~20%). Loss is largest when numbers and "object" entities are removed and for larger models. Re-identification rates do not reliably predict information loss. They "challenge the default reliance on full anonymization". Limitations: short (259-day) large-cap-tilted sample, contemporaneous returns, one prompt design. — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15364v1)
- Ludwig et al.: entity masking plus date restriction did not stop verbatim headline completion (73 vs 60 exact reproductions); "its wider applicability is unclear". — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)
- FinCAD's model-ranking test: anonymisation (placeholders for tickers/names) *lowered* the alignment between in-sample and true out-of-sample model rankings (Spearman ρ̄ = +0.547 vs baseline +0.779), as did prompt-injection (+0.482); FinCAD raised it (+0.846). — [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564)
- He et al. describe masking (Engelberg et al.; Glasserman & Lin) as "robust methods for isolating temporal information" but argue pretraining from scratch is "a more comprehensive solution" than making a model forget or infer from context. — [arXiv 2510.11677](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11677)
- Numbers-only standardization (Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev): removing names and replacing years with relative labels on Compustat-format statements gives 0.07% firm and 2.95% year re-identification. — [arXiv 2407.17866v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17866v2)

**(2) Point-in-time / chronologically consistent models**

- **StoriesLM** (Sarkar 2024): BERT-style encoders trained on the American Stories historical-newspaper corpus; v1 starts with 1900 data and each subsequent model continues the previous checkpoint with one more year up to 1963; v2 is described as a set of 125 encoder-only models with time-indexed cutoffs. Limitation: historical newspaper language; scores lowest in He et al.'s comparisons (GLUE 79.36; Sharpe 3.42). — [HF v1 card](https://huggingface.co/StoriesLM/StoriesLM-v1-1963); [HF v2 card](https://huggingface.co/suproteem/StoriesLM-v2-1928); [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3)
- **Time Machine GPT (TiMaGPT)** (Drinkall et al., Findings of NAACL 2024): a series of point-in-time GPT-style models designed to be "nonprognosticative" (no future facts or language change); models and training sets public. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.208)
- **FinText** (Rahimikia & Drinkall 2024): year-specific finance/accounting language models pre-trained on data from 2007 to 2023; secondary summaries report they outperform much larger LLaMA models in trading tests (equal-weighted Sharpe 3.45). He et al. note these use proprietary time-stamped financial text and were not evaluated on general language understanding. [V-snip] — [newsletter summary](https://theaifinancefrontier.beehiiv.com/p/re-visiting-large-language-models-in-finance); [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3)
- **ChronoBERT / ChronoGPT** (He, Lv, Manela & Wu 2025): 26 yearly vintages 1999–2024. ChronoBERT = ModernBERT architecture, 149M parameters, 1,024-token context (ChronoBERT-1999 trained on 460B tokens); ChronoGPT = modified nanoGPT, ~1.5B parameters, 1,792-token context. Leakage validation: 0 of 76 post-cutoff major-event tokens predicted; no vintage predicts a future first-term president. GLUE: ChronoBERT-1999 84.71, ChronoBERT-2024 85.54 vs BERT 84.52, StoriesLM 79.36, FinBERT 76.41, Llama 3.1 86.31. Limitations: capability plateaus (ChronoGPT HellaSwag ~37% vs GPT-2 XL 48%), residual date errors (e.g., OCR) can admit future text. Models: huggingface.co/manelalab. — [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3)
- **ChronoGPT-Instruct** (He et al. 2025): instruction-tuned vintages (1999, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, 2024 + realtime) on 425,119 prompt–response pairs screened by GPT-4.1 for time-sensitivity; 0 of 73 post-cutoff presidents and 0 of 76 post-cutoff events predicted; but Alpaca win-rates against Qwen-1.5-1.8B-Chat are only 12.59%–16.79%. — [arXiv 2510.11677](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11677)
- **NoLBERT** (Kakhbod & Li 2025): DeBERTa-v3-based, 109M parameters, pre-trained only on 1976–1995 text (validated on 1996) to avoid both look-ahead and "lookback" bias (semantic drift); tested with paired t-tests on 20 meaning-shifted words per bias type; applied to patent text to build firm innovation networks. — [arXiv 2509.01110](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2509.01110)
- **DatedGPT** (Yan et al. 2026): twelve 1.3B-parameter decoder models trained from scratch on ~100B tokens each (FineWeb-Edu filtered by crawl year), annual cutoffs 2013–2024, plus instruction-tuned variants; perplexity probing shows a reversal at each cutoff; benchmark average 40.1–42.7 vs SmolLM-1.7B-Instruct 41.1. Limitations: crawl timestamps "do not reflect the true creation date"; finance instruction data generated by Llama-3.3-70B; checkpoints promised on acceptance; demo at datedgpt.com. Cutoffs start in 2013, so it cannot cover Enron/WorldCom/Lehman. — [arXiv 2603.11838v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11838v1)
- **PiT-Inference "Pitinf"** models: proprietary commercial point-in-time LLMs (Small <10B, Medium <100B, Large >500B parameters; effective cutoff Jan 2020 in the benchmark); in Look-Ahead-Bench they show alpha decay of +0.31 to +1.30 pp vs −15 to −22 pp for Llama 3.1/DeepSeek. Limitations: five tech stocks, two six-month windows, proprietary models (limited reproducibility); the benchmark author self-describes it as a proof of concept. — [arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13770)
- Ludwig et al.: open-weight models with fixed, documented training cutoffs let researchers make the leakage term zero "by design" by sampling evaluation data after the cutoff; closed models do not (undisclosed data, possible continuous fine-tuning, month-to-month output drift). — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)

**(3) Strict post-cutoff out-of-sample evaluation; live benchmarks**

- Lopez-Lira et al.: "evaluations of LLMs' forecasting capabilities should focus exclusively on data beyond their training cutoff"; small post-cutoff "robustness checks" are statistically too weak to distinguish skill from memorization (their Proposition 2). — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Halawi et al. (2024): test set of 914 forecasting-platform questions opened after 1 June 2023 — after the cutoffs of GPT-4-1106 (Apr 2023) and GPT-3.5 (Sep 2021) — "preventing leakage from pre-training"; system Brier .240 vs human crowd .247 as summarized in search results [V-snip for numbers]. — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563)
- ForecastBench (Karger et al., ICLR 2025): a dynamic benchmark of 1,000 automatically generated, regularly updated questions "about future events that have no known answer at the time of submission"; on a 200-question subset expert forecasters beat the best LLM (p < 0.001). — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea74e45a229dac70b5b63b28d8934db6-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Paleka et al. (ICLR 2026): evaluation of LLM forecasters is undermined by many forms of temporal leakage — mis-dated documents, retrieval systems trained on or re-ranked with future data, assuming models know nothing after their stated cutoff, and back-generated questions that reveal answers; and results may not extrapolate to real-world forecasting. — [arXiv 2506.00723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723v1)
- Zhang & Stadie (2026): the pre/post-cutoff contrast is confounded by recency; leakage is identified only with external information — (i) regression discontinuity at a known cutoff, (ii) difference-in-differences against a matched clean control model, or (iii) paraphrase probes (detection only). Validation with planted leakage: inflation is null at dose 0 (−0.003) and +0.063 at dose 64; leakage concentrates on surprising, well-covered outcomes; partial extraction (w = 0.5) yields 75% of full-memorization inflation. Nulls should be reported as bounds with stated power. — [arXiv 2608.02985](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02985)
- Cutoff dates themselves are uncertain: effective cutoffs differ from reported ones (Cheng et al.), by up to eight months earlier and 22 months across vendors (HindsightBench); closed models may be silently updated (Ludwig et al.; Kong et al.). — [arXiv 2403.12958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12958); [arXiv 2607.18867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867); [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233)
- EDINET-Bench (fraud detection) states a fully clean test "would require" annual reports released after each model's cutoff and provides tooling to refresh the dataset for that purpose. — [arXiv 2506.08762](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08762)

**(4) Prompt-based "pretend you don't know" / simulated cutoffs**

- Lopez-Lira et al. (GPT-4o, GDP growth, artificial cutoff Dec 2010): with a system-prompt-only fake cutoff, threshold accuracy is 97.59% before and 98.00% after the fake cutoff (1 refusal in 50) versus ~40% true post-cutoff accuracy; with system + user prompts the model mostly refuses (38 of 51) but is still 69.23% accurate when it answers. Conclusion: prompts cannot change encoded information. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Ludwig et al.: told to ignore information after a bill's introduction, GPT-4o still predicted outcomes accurately and reproduced 330 (vs 344) bill descriptions verbatim. — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)
- Sarkar & Vafa "discuss the limitations of prompting-based approaches to counteract this bias". [V-snip] — [ICML 2025 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/48685)
- Gao et al. (EMNLP 2025), "Can Prompts Rewind Time for LLMs?": prompted cutoffs achieve average "unlearn success" of ~82.5% on the Factual subset (675 items) and ~70.0% on Semantic (303), but only ~19.2% on Counterfactual (689) — i.e., when the forgotten knowledge is not asked directly but is causally related to the query. Per model on Counterfactual: GPT-4o 0.144/0.201, DeepSeek-V3 0.152/0.125; reasoning models do better (DeepSeek-R1 0.723/0.701, o3 0.478/0.533). — [arXiv 2510.02340v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02340v2)
- Eliseev & Seleznev (2026): prompt-sensitivity "fake date tests" for macro forecasting; the abstract as summarized indicates the tested modern LLMs' in-sample forecasts exhibit look-ahead bias under the first fake-date test (wording of the summary was garbled; re-read the PDF). — [arXiv 2601.07992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07992)
- FinCAD: strengthening the system-prompt look-ahead disclaimer ("prompt injection" baseline) reduced, rather than improved, ranking alignment (ρ̄ = +0.482 vs +0.779). — [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564)
- Kong et al.: "declaring a knowledge cutoff date" and filtering retrieved documents by publication time are listed as common fixes that fail, because cutoffs are opaque and retrieved documents are edited/re-ranked using future information. — [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233)

**(5) Inference-time unlearning, decoding and steering corrections (2025–2026)**

- Merchant & Levy (2025), **Divergence Decoding**: adjust the logits of a large frozen base model with two small models — a "forget" model q and a "retain" model p: l̂ = l_P + α·(l_q − l_p) (equivalent to a product of experts), or a rank-based top-k exclusion. Finance tests use Gemma-3-27b-it with fine-tuned Gemma-3-4b-it pairs on 41 M&A deals and an airline example; MMLU stays at 77.58 (α = 2) vs 77.94 baseline; on MUSE it is reported to match or beat GradDiff/NPO/SimNPO (numbers only in figures). Limitations: sensitivity to instruction-format mismatch between small and base models; extra forward passes; no comparison with prompting. A production system is advertised (frontiertopit.com). — [arXiv 2512.06607](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06607)
- Li, Wang & Ma (2026), **FinCAD**: inference-time Context-Aware Decoding with (i) an adversarially discovered model-specific "memory-activation" prompt (MIPROv2/DSPy, calibrated on S&P 500 direction labels 2005–2015) and (ii) an entity- and date-adaptive strength α(s,t). Reduces in-sample returns where memory is strong, leaves post-cutoff results unchanged (Phi-4: ᾱ_OOS = 0), general benchmarks within +2.2 to −4.3 pp. Limitations stated: partial mitigation, heuristic entropy proxy with no counterfactual ground truth, only 7–14B open models, needs logits (not usable with text-only APIs), price-only single-stock backtests. — [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564)
- Merchant & Levy (2026), **feature steering**: amplify sparse-autoencoder features (Gemma 3 27B with Gemma Scope 2; Qwen) associated with time-aware reasoning; only one of five candidate features was "causally useful"; no usable suppression feature for look-ahead was found; authors position steering as a component, not a complete solution. — [arXiv 2606.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27199v1)
- Lopez-Lira et al.: black-box fine-tuning/unlearning to "forget" is theoretically non-identified without white-box verification (their Corollary 2). — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Diagnostics rather than cures: LAP interaction test (Gao, Jiang & Yan; needs first-token log-probabilities only); HindsightBench four-arm date manipulation (revealed / date-only / masked / transplanted) with outcome-recall and date-recovery probes, probe-level access only; MemGuard-Alpha shows membership-inference filtering is not reliable enough to act on. — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847); [arXiv 2607.18867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867); [arXiv 2603.26797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797)

### Inferences
- For a fraud agent reading FY2000–FY2007 10-Ks, remedies rank as follows on *validity*: point-in-time model with vintage ≤ filing year (ChronoGPT/ChronoBERT cover 1999+, so FY2000 Enron, FY2001 WorldCom and FY2007 Lehman are all inside their range) > inference-time unlearning on an open-weight model (no guarantee) > entity neutering with a re-identification audit (upper bound only; likely to fail for these firms) > prompt instructions (shown not to work for indirect/causal knowledge — exactly the "is this firm risky?" case).
- The validity ranking is nearly the reverse of the *capability* ranking: Chrono models have 1,024–1,792-token contexts and weak instruction-following, so they can score chunks/embeddings of a 10-K but cannot act as a reasoning agent over a full filing. A hybrid design is implied by the literature: frontier model for the agentic pipeline + a point-in-time model (or embeddings + supervised head trained only on pre-date labels) as a leakage-free benchmark, with the gap reported as an upper bound on contamination (Engelberg-style).
- Anonymizing a 10-K is intrinsically harder than anonymizing a headline: business description, segment names, auditor, geography, executive names, unique transactions (e.g., SPE structures) are all identifying; Wu et al. show removing numbers and "object" entities is exactly what destroys information. Expect a steep leakage/information trade-off.
- Gao et al.'s counterfactual-subset result (≈19% success) is the most relevant prompt-cutoff evidence for fraud scoring, since the scandal is not asked about directly but is causally linked to the requested judgment.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found on whether any remedy works for *long-document* inputs of 10-K length other than Engelberg et al.'s MD&A/earnings-call extension (numbers not accessible).
- Divergence Decoding and FinCAD report no head-to-head comparison with each other or with point-in-time models; neither has been tested on frontier-scale closed models or on fraud/bankruptcy labels.
- TiMaGPT's and FinText's year ranges and sizes were not verified from primary text; whether any public point-in-time model has a long (≥8k) context window is unknown.
- DatedGPT's public checkpoint availability as of Nov 2026 was not confirmed.

## Key Question 4 — Fraud / misstatement / bankruptcy detection from filings with LLMs: is look-ahead bias or memorization of known scandals addressed?

### Takeaway
I found **no paper that directly tests whether an LLM's fraud-risk score for a known scandal firm (Enron, WorldCom, Lehman, etc.) is driven by memorized knowledge of the scandal**. The LLM fraud-detection literature mostly worries about a different leakage — the same company appearing in train and test — and, at best, runs light contamination checks (company-name guessing, performance-by-year). This is an open gap that the project can claim.

### Cited Findings
- **EDINET-Bench** (Sugiura et al., Sakana AI; arXiv:2506.08762, v2 Mar 2026): accounting-fraud detection on Japanese annual reports (EDINET, Apr 2014–Apr 2025; 534 fraud / 555 non-fraud reports, fraud labels derived from amended reports classified with Claude 3.7 Sonnet). Best LLM (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, all inputs) ROC-AUC 0.73 ± 0.02, MCC 0.32; a Random Forest on summary data reaches 0.75 / 0.38. **Contamination checks:** (a) company-name prediction from tabular statements — accuracy ≤ 0.05 for all models (GPT-4o 0.000, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 0.050); (b) performance by fiscal year — "no declining trend for recent years". Conclusion: contamination "unlikely to be affecting our evaluation", but "we cannot conclude that the dataset is entirely free of contamination"; a clean test needs post-cutoff reports. — [arXiv 2506.08762](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08762)
- **Company-Isolated FSFD** (Waffo Dzuyo, Guibon, Cerisara & Belmar-Letelier; arXiv:2607.19259, FinLLM@IJCAI 2026): SEC 10-Q financials 2009–2024 + summarized MD&A + AAER labels (10,159 samples, 511 fraud). Under random 5-fold splits each of the 321 fraudulent firms appears in 3.35 folds on average; AUC for fine-tuned Llama-3.1 8B falls from 0.96 (random split) to 0.68 (company-isolated), F1 from 0.76 to 0.14; zero-shot LLM AUC ≈ 0.47–0.52. The split "does not strictly enforce chronological sequencing"; pretraining memorization of known fraud firms, knowledge cutoffs and name anonymization are **not discussed**. — [arXiv 2607.19259](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19259)
- **FraudDebate-Agent** (Yue, Yang & Liu, *Mathematics* 14(15):2695, 2026): multi-agent LLM debate on AAER-labelled firm-years linked to EDGAR-CORPUS; framed as "a fraud-risk screening and risk-ranking tool … rather than a determination of fraudulent intent"; notes AAER labels are right-censored (enforcement arrives years after the misstatement). Evaluation split and any memorization control could not be verified (MDPI 403). [V-snip] — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/15/2695); [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jmathe/v14y2026i15p2695-d2000448.html)
- **Bhattacharya & Mickovic (2024), "Accounting fraud detection using contextual language learning"**: fine-tuned BERT on 10-K MD&A; reported 15% and 12% improvements over text and quantitative benchmarks (encoder model with a generic pretraining cutoff; look-ahead in pretraining not the focus). [V-snip] — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381068994_Accounting_fraud_detection_using_contextual_language_learning)
- **DeepFraud, "Financial statement fraud detection via large language models"**: FinBERT/FinLlama/FinGPT embeddings of temporal MD&A changes, 1995–2024; no leakage/memorization controls mentioned in the abstract. [V-snip] — [CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/outputs/694287139)
- **AuditAgent** (arXiv:2510.00156, ICAIF '25 submission): multi-agent fraud-evidence localization using China Securities Regulatory Commission enforcement records; leakage analysis not visible in abstract. [V-snip] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00156v1)
- **FinFRE-RAG** (Tan, Ma & Zhang, ACL 2026; arXiv:2512.13040) concerns tabular transaction-fraud datasets, not filings; no memorization/temporal-split discussion in the abstract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13040)
- **FinVerBench** (Panda 2026; arXiv:2605.29586): LLM verification of numerical consistency in 10-K XBRL statements of 43 S&P 500 firms using *injected* errors; nine of fourteen models flag 95–100% of clean statements as erroneous under the guided prompt. (Injecting synthetic errors is a design that sidesteps memorized outcomes; memorization itself is not discussed in the abstract.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29586)
- Closest accounting evidence on memorization comes from earnings prediction, not fraud: Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev's name/year-guessing and post-cutoff tests on anonymized numeric statements, and Levy's JAR 2026 finding of significant look-ahead bias in commercial LLMs. — [arXiv 2407.17866v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17866v2); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v64y2026i3p1139-1188.html)
- Kong et al.'s survey of 164 LLM-for-finance papers (2023–2025): only 26.8% acknowledge look-ahead bias and 1.2% survivorship bias. — [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233)

### Inferences
- Evaluation designs available "off the shelf" for a fraud-from-10-K study, assembled from the above: (i) **firm/year re-identification probe** on the (anonymized) filing (Kim et al.; EDINET-Bench; Lopez-Lira et al.; Wu et al.); (ii) **outcome-recall probe** — firm name + fiscal year only, ask for fraud/restatement/bankruptcy outcome, and use the recall propensity as an interaction term à la LAP (Gao, Jiang & Yan); (iii) **company-isolated and chronological splits** for any trained component (CI-FSFD); (iv) **post-cutoff hold-out** of recent AAER/restatement/bankruptcy cases, acknowledging AAER right-censoring; (v) **synthetic/injected-misstatement counterfactuals** on clean filings (FinVerBench style) or digit perturbation (Levy), which have no memorized label; (vi) **point-in-time model benchmark** (ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT vintage ≤ fiscal year) as a leakage-free comparator; (vii) **fame-stratified analysis** — compare scores for notorious vs obscure fraud firms and matched non-fraud peers, since leakage concentrates on "surprising, well-covered outcomes" (Zhang & Stadie) and large firms (Lopez-Lira et al.; FinCAD).
- Showcase cases such as Enron FY2000, WorldCom FY2001 and Lehman FY2007 should be treated as *contaminated illustrations*, not as evidence of detection ability; any headline accuracy claim should rest on obscure and/or post-cutoff cases.
- The name-guessing result in EDINET-Bench (≤5% from tables) and Kim et al. (0.07%) show that *numeric tables alone* rarely identify a firm, whereas narrative sections do; a design that separates a "numbers-only anonymized" arm from a "full-text" arm would bracket the contamination.

### Gaps
- No study found that probes LLM recall of AAER/restatement/bankruptcy outcomes by firm-year, or that reports fraud-score differences between famous and obscure fraud firms.
- No LLM bankruptcy-prediction-from-10-K paper addressing look-ahead bias was found (a Nov 2025 foundation-model bankruptcy paper, arXiv:2511.16375, surfaced in search but its snippets mention no memorization controls; not read).
- FraudDebate-Agent, DeepFraud, AuditAgent and Bhattacharya & Mickovic were not read in full; their split designs are unverified.

## Key Question 5 — State of the art 2025–2026 and consensus on best practice

### Takeaway
By late 2026 there is broad agreement that (a) parametric look-ahead bias is real, heterogeneous, and worst for prominent entities; (b) prompt instructions and naive name masking are insufficient; (c) the only assumption-free designs are post-cutoff/live evaluation or models whose training data is time-stamped before the evaluation period; and (d) researchers must run and report application-specific memorization diagnostics. Disagreement remains on *magnitude* (modest in He et al. and Wu et al.; large in Lopez-Lira et al., Levy, FinCAD, Look-Ahead-Bench) and on whether anonymization's information loss outweighs its benefit. The newest work (mid/late 2026) turns to inference-time debiasing and to more careful identification of leakage vs recency.

### Cited Findings
- Consensus that prompts fail: Lopez-Lira et al. (fake cutoff accuracy 97.6% → 98.0%), Ludwig et al. (330 of 344 verbatim completions persist), Sarkar & Vafa, Kong et al., FinCAD's prompt-injection baseline; partial exception for directly asked facts in Gao et al. (82.5% factual vs 19.2% counterfactual). — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2); [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4); [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233); [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564); [arXiv 2510.02340v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02340v2)
- Recommended practice (Lopez-Lira et al.): evaluate only beyond the training cutoff; prefer temporally consistent models or open-source models with documented temporal training; run memorization tests on one's own research data to get a lower bound on contamination; do not rely on prompt-based cutoffs or anonymization alone. — [arXiv 2504.14765v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- Recommended practice (Ludwig et al.): use open-weight models with documented cutoffs and sample evaluation data after them; alternatively use confidential documents with credible provenance; be explicit about the target population and sampling relative to plausible training corpora; treat complete-population analyses as descriptive. — [arXiv 2412.07031v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07031v4)
- Recommended practice (Kong et al., "temporal sanitation"): disclose the latest date covered by pre-training/fine-tuning and make the evaluation window respect it; build retrieval corpora only from documents available at time t using archived snapshots with verifiable as-of timestamps; use point-in-time structured data; log prompts, retrieved documents and timestamps; screen outputs for references to post-t events; validate on open-source models. — [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233)
- Recommended practice (Zhang & Stadie): do not treat a flat pre/post profile as a clean bill of health; pick one defensible external reference (known cutoff → RD; matched clean control → DiD), report adjusted scores with confidence intervals and assumptions, and report nulls as bounds with stated power. — [arXiv 2608.02985](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02985)
- Magnitude is contested: "modest" (He et al.: ChronoGPT 4.92 vs Llama 3.1 4.90 Sharpe); limited relative to anonymization loss (Wu et al.); versus substantial (Gao et al.: +32% amplification per SD of LAP; Look-Ahead-Bench: −15 to −22 pp alpha decay; FinCAD: up to −67.1% in-sample return correction; Levy: "may account for a large share" of predictive ability). — [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3); [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15364v1); [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847); [arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13770); [arXiv 2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v64y2026i3p1139-1188.html)
- State of the art in point-in-time models (Nov 2026): open — ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT(-Instruct) 1999–2024 (149M / ~1.5B), DatedGPT 2013–2024 (1.3B), NoLBERT (109M, 1976–1995), StoriesLM (1900–1963), TiMaGPT, FinText (2007–2023); commercial — PiT-Inference Pitinf models (up to >500B parameters claimed) and a production system based on Merchant & Levy's divergence decoding. — [arXiv 2502.21206v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21206v3); [arXiv 2603.11838v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11838v1); [arXiv 2509.01110](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2509.01110); [arXiv 2601.13770](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13770); [arXiv 2512.06607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06607)
- State of the art in diagnostics: LAP interaction test (Gao, Jiang & Yan), HindsightBench audit protocol, FinCutBench knowledge-boundary benchmark, fake-date tests (Eliseev & Seleznev), Look-Ahead-Bench; membership-inference filtering judged unreliable (MemGuard-Alpha). — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847); [arXiv 2607.18867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867); [arXiv 2611.11588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11588); [arXiv 2601.07992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07992); [arXiv 2603.26797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797)
- Adoption is still low: 26.8% of 164 reviewed LLM-for-finance papers acknowledge look-ahead bias; 74% of surveyed practitioners/researchers (76% in the appendix) say ready-to-use evaluation tools are scarce or non-existent. — [arXiv 2602.14233](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233)
- The topic has reached top accounting/econ outlets and venues: Levy in *Journal of Accounting Research* (2026), Didisheim et al. in *Economics Letters* (2025), Ludwig et al. as NBER WP 33344, Paleka et al. at ICLR 2026, Gao et al. at EMNLP 2025, DatedGPT listed at NeurIPS 2026. — [IDEAS/RePEc (Levy)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v64y2026i3p1139-1188.html); [IDEAS/RePEc (Didisheim)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v256y2025ics0165176525004392.html); [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/paleka2026iclr-pitfalls/); [NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175082)

### Inferences
- A defensible best-practice stack for the fraud project, synthesized from the above (not stated as a single checklist in any one paper): (1) declare the model's documented and, where possible, empirically probed cutoff; (2) make the *primary* quantitative claim on cases whose outcome became public after that cutoff, or on a point-in-time model; (3) for pre-cutoff showcase cases, report re-identification and outcome-recall probe results and treat scores as upper bounds; (4) run an anonymized arm and report both the re-identification rate and the information loss; (5) include injected/synthetic-misstatement counterfactuals; (6) stratify by firm prominence; (7) never rely on "ignore what you know" instructions as the control; (8) log everything with as-of timestamps, including any retrieval.
- The field is moving from "detect and avoid" (2023–2025) to "measure and adjust" (2026: adjusted scores, decoding-time corrections). None of the adjustment methods has yet been validated on classification-of-entities tasks like fraud scoring, so using them would itself need validation (e.g., planted-leakage tests as in Zhang & Stadie).

### Gaps
- No formal consensus statement or reporting standard (journal policy, checklist adopted by a top journal) was found; the "consensus" above is an inference from overlapping recommendations in independent papers.
- Several 2026 items are single-author or early preprints with small samples (Look-Ahead-Bench: 5 stocks; HindsightBench; FinVerBench; MemGuard-Alpha) and should be cited as preliminary.
- Publication status of Sarkar & Vafa, Glasserman & Lin, Engelberg et al., He et al., Lopez-Lira et al. and Kim et al. in peer-reviewed journals could not be confirmed as of Nov 2026 (all located only as working papers / preprints / workshop papers).
