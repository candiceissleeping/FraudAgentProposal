## 1. Entity bias and knowledge conflicts (context vs. parametric memory): how is "the model relied on memory rather than the context" operationalized and measured?

### Takeaway
The field's standard operationalization is a **counterfactual-context test**: take an instance the model answers correctly from memory, edit the context so that it supports a different answer (entity substitution, or an LLM-written "counter-memory" passage), and count how often the model still outputs the memorized answer — the **memorization ratio M_R = p_o / (p_o + p_s)** of Longpre et al. (2021). Later work adds distributional measures (persuasion/susceptibility scores), behavioural taxonomies (stubborn vs. receptive, confirmation bias), and mechanistic measures (memory heads vs. context heads, a 1-D "context-vs-prior" direction). Two robust regularities matter for a 10-K fraud scorer: (i) prior reliance rises with **entity popularity/familiarity**, and (ii) models are most stubborn when the context contains *some* text consistent with the prior (confirmation bias), which is exactly the situation of a famous firm's filing.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational behavioural definitions**
- **Longpre, Perisetla, Chen, Ramesh, DuBois, Singh (2021), "Entity-Based Knowledge Conflicts in Question Answering", EMNLP 2021, arXiv:2109.05052 [V].** Method: build "substitution instances" by replacing the gold answer entity in the passage (corpus substitution, type-swap substitution, popularity substitution, alias substitution), evaluate only on examples the model originally answered correctly, and record the share of Original (p_o), Substitute (p_s) and Other predictions; M_R = p_o/(p_o+p_s). Findings: generative QA readers predict the substitute "rarely more than 50% of the time" for the NQ model and revert to the original answer "up to 20% of the time for NQ, and 75% for NewsQA"; M_R rises with model size ("from < 15% to ≥ 50%" on Train/Dev-AO across T5 small→XXL); there is an inverse relation between training-retriever quality and M_R; more popular substitute entities are more readily accepted. Mitigation: training with substituted instances reduces memorization to negligible levels and "improves out-of-distribution generalization by 4%-7%". Limitations: fine-tuned T5/extractive readers, not instruction-following LLMs; substituted passages can be incoherent (criticised by Xie et al.). — [arXiv 2109.05052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05052)
- **Xie, Zhang, Chen, Lou, Su (2023; ICLR 2024 Spotlight), "Adaptive Chameleon or Stubborn Sloth: Revealing the Behavior of Large Language Models in Knowledge Conflicts", arXiv:2305.13300 [V].** Method: elicit each LLM's own parametric memory closed-book (PopQA, StrategyQA), have ChatGPT write a coherent "counter-memory" passage, filter with an NLI entailment check and an answer-consistency check. Findings: with a single coherent counter-memory passage LLMs are "highly receptive" (contradicting substitution-based results, which the authors attribute to incoherent substituted text); but when parametric-memory-consistent and conflicting evidence are both present LLMs show "strong confirmation bias"; confirmation bias grows with popularity (GPT-4 reaches ~80% memorization ratio on the most popular questions); answers are sensitive to evidence order (e.g., Llama2-7B 33.3% vs 82.8% on PopQA depending on which evidence comes first) and evidence quantity (ChatGPT on PopQA 3.7% → 99.8% as memory-supporting passages go 0/2 → 2/2). Limitations: two QA datasets; counter-memory generated by ChatGPT; no dedicated limitations section. (Order/quantity numbers extracted from the HTML tables by an automated reader — re-check against Tables 5–6 before quoting.) — [arXiv 2305.13300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300)
- **Chen, Zhang, Choi (2022), "Rich Knowledge Sources Bring Complex Knowledge Conflicts: Recalibrating Models to Reflect Conflicting Evidence", EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2210.13701 [V, bibliographic only]** — extends Longpre's setting to multiple passages. — [arXiv 2210.13701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13701)
- **Neeman, Aharoni, Honovich, Choshen, Szpektor, Abend (2022; ACL 2023), "DisentQA: Disentangling Parametric and Contextual Knowledge with Counterfactual Question Answering", arXiv:2211.05655 [V-abs].** Method: train (counterfactual data augmentation) a QA model to emit *two* answers — a contextual one and a parametric one. Finding (abstract): improves robustness to knowledge conflicts on Natural Questions. Limitation: requires fine-tuning; not an inference-time method. — [arXiv 2211.05655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05655)
- **Li, Rawat, Zaheer, Wang, Lukasik, Veit, Yu, Kumar (2022; ACL 2023 [Findings status M]), "Large Language Models with Controllable Working Memory", arXiv:2211.05110 [V-abs].** Defines *controllability* (follow relevant context that conflicts with memory) and *robustness* (ignore irrelevant context). Finding: T5 and PaLM "could exhibit poor controllability and robustness, which do not scale with increasing model size"; proposes Knowledge Aware FineTuning (KAFT) with counterfactual and irrelevant contexts. Limitation: fine-tuning-based. — [arXiv 2211.05110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05110)
- **Xu, Qi, Guo, Wang, Wang, Zhang, Xu (2024), "Knowledge Conflicts for LLMs: A Survey", EMNLP 2024 (main), arXiv:2403.08319 [V].** Taxonomy: context-memory, inter-context and intra-memory conflict; solutions grouped by objective — *faithful to context*, *discriminating misinformation* (faithful to memory), *disentangling sources*, *improving factuality* — and by timing (pre-hoc vs. post-hoc). — [arXiv 2403.08319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08319), [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.486.pdf)

**Entity bias as a named phenomenon (relation extraction / reading comprehension lineage)**
- **Peng, Gao, Han, Lin, Li, Liu, Sun, Zhou (2020), "Learning from Context or Names? An Empirical Study on Neural Relation Extraction", EMNLP 2020, arXiv:2010.01923 [V-abs].** RE models "heavily rely on the information from entity mentions", and datasets "may leak shallow heuristics via entity mentions"; proposes entity-masked contrastive pre-training. — [arXiv 2010.01923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01923)
- **Wang, Chen, Zhou, Cai, Liang, Liu, Yang, Liu, Hooi (2022), "Should We Rely on Entity Mentions for Relation Extraction? Debiasing Relation Extraction with Counterfactual Analysis", NAACL 2022, arXiv:2205.03784 [V-abs].** CoRE: inference-time counterfactual analysis on a causal graph that subtracts the entity-only effect; notes that entity masking "degrades the RE performance because it loses the semantic information of entities". — [arXiv 2205.03784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03784)
- **Yan, Xiao, Mukherjee, Lin, Jia, Ren (2021; NAACL 2022), "On the Robustness of Reading Comprehension Models to Entity Renaming", arXiv:2110.08555 [V-abs].** Entity-renaming audit: replace names with names from the same test set, common names, or *arbitrary strings*; "MRC models consistently perform worse when entities are renamed", with large drops on distantly supervised datasets. — [arXiv 2110.08555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08555)
- **Wang, Mo, Wang, Zhou, Chen (2023), "A Causal View of Entity Bias in (Large) Language Models", Findings of EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2305.14695 [V].** Structural causal model in which the entity name carries both *predictive* and *biasing* information; intervention = perturb the entity with **neighboring entities**. Black-box (GPT-3.5) version is a four-step in-context intervention: (1) replace the entity with a placeholder such as [ENTITY]; (2) ask the LLM for k similar entities; (3) define the placeholder as "can be any of E_o, E_a, E_b" (original + neighbors, shuffled); (4) prepend the definition and query. Findings: white-box training-time intervention improves OOD performance by 5.7 points (RE) and 9.1 points (MRC); black-box intervention gives "up to 20.5 points of improvement of exact match accuracy on MRC and up to 17.6 points of reduction in memorization ratio on RE". Ablation: placeholder *without* a definition drives M_R to 0 but F1 falls "from 71.8 points to 37.9 points" — pure masking destroys needed information. Limitations: GPT-3.5 only; RE/MRC tasks; needs entity recognition and k as a tuning knob; the original entity is still named inside the definition. — [arXiv 2305.14695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14695)

**Prompt- and decoding-level measurement/mitigation**
- **Zhou, Zhang, Poon, Chen (2023), "Context-faithful Prompting for Large Language Models", Findings of EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2303.11315 [V].** Measures M_R on counterfactual NQ (Longpre-style) and Re-TACRED plus abstention accuracy. Templates: base; attributed ("…based on the given text?"); instruction-based (APE-generated instruction); **opinion-based** ("Bob said, '{c}' … in Bob's opinion?"); opinion+instruction; plus counterfactual demonstrations. Findings: "reducing the memorization ratio of text-davinci-003 from 35.2% to 3.0% on natural questions"; with opinion+instruction and counterfactual demonstrations, a 32.2% reduction in M_R for MRC and 10.9% for RE on GPT-3.5, and 39.4% / 57.3% on LLaMA-2-7B-chat; opinion-based prompts outperform plain instructed prompts; original (factual) demonstrations "may even impair faithfulness". Limitation: short QA/RE contexts; the opinion framing changes the question from "what is true" to "what does the text say". — [arXiv 2303.11315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11315)
- **Shi, Han, Lewis, Tsvetkov, Zettlemoyer, Yih (2023; NAACL 2024), "Trusting Your Evidence: Hallucinate Less with Context-aware Decoding", arXiv:2305.14739 [V].** CAD samples from softmax[(1+α)·logit(y | c, x) − α·logit(y | x)], i.e., contrasts with- vs. without-context distributions. Findings: 14.3% factuality gain for LLaMA on summarization; "a 2.9x improvement to LLaMA-30B on a knowledge conflicts QA dataset (Longpre et al., 2021)"; evaluated on MemoTrap and NQ-Swap. Limitations: needs logits (not available on most closed APIs); two forward passes; a static α over-corrects when there is no conflict (see AdaCAD). — [arXiv 2305.14739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14739)
- **Wang, Prasad, Stengel-Eskin, Bansal (2024; NAACL 2025), "AdaCAD: Adaptively Decoding to Balance Conflicts between Contextual and Parametric Knowledge", arXiv:2409.07394 [V-abs].** Sets the contrast weight per instance from the Jensen–Shannon divergence between contextual and parametric distributions; "average QA accuracy gains of 14.21% (absolute) over a static contrastive baseline"; static contrastive baselines "hurt performance when conflict is absent". — [arXiv 2409.07394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07394)
- **Yuan, Yang, Wang, Liu, Zhao, Liu (2024), "Discerning and Resolving Knowledge Conflicts through Adaptive Decoding with Contextual Information-Entropy Constraint" (COIECD), Findings of ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.11893 [V, bibliographic only].** — [arXiv 2402.11893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11893)
- **Wu, Qiu, Ross, Akyürek, Chen, Wang, Kim, Andreas, Kim (2023; NAACL 2024), "Reasoning or Reciting? Exploring the Capabilities and Limitations of Language Models Through Counterfactual Tasks", arXiv:2307.02477 [V-abs].** Counterfactual *task* variants (deviating from default assumptions) across 11 tasks: "nontrivial performance on the counterfactual variants, but … performance substantially and consistently degrades compared to the default conditions" — evidence that default-world priors drive behaviour. — [arXiv 2307.02477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02477)
- **McCoy, Yao, Friedman, Hardy, Griffiths (2023), "Embers of Autoregression: Understanding Large Language Models Through the Problem They are Trained to Solve", arXiv:2309.13638 [V-abs; PNAS 2024 publication M].** LLM accuracy depends on the probability of the task, target output and input "even in deterministic settings where probability should not matter" (GPT-3.5/GPT-4, eleven tasks). — [arXiv 2309.13638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13638)

**Quantitative / distributional measures of prior reliance**
- **Du, Snæbjarnarson, Stoehr, White, Schein, Cotterell (2024), "Context versus Prior Knowledge in Language Models", ACL 2024, arXiv:2404.04633 [V-abs].** Two mutual-information metrics: **persuasion score** of a context and **susceptibility score** of an entity ("how much the model can be swayed away from its original answer distribution about an entity"); hypothesis and evidence that models "rely more on prior knowledge for questions about entities … that they are more familiar with due to higher exposure in the training corpus". — [arXiv 2404.04633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04633)
- **Wu, Wu, Zou (2024), "ClashEval: Quantifying the tug-of-war between an LLM's internal prior and external evidence", NeurIPS 2024 (Datasets & Benchmarks track [track M]), arXiv:2404.10198 [V-abs].** 1,200+ questions in six domains with graded perturbations of the retrieved content; six LLMs incl. GPT-4o adopt incorrect retrieved content "over 60% of the time", but less so the more unrealistic the content, and the less confident the model's prior (token probability) the more it follows the context. — [arXiv 2404.10198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198)
- **Kortukov, Rubinstein, Nguyen, Oh (2024), "Studying Large Language Model Behaviors Under Context-Memory Conflicts With Real Documents", arXiv:2404.16032 [V-abs; venue not verified].** With *real* conflicting documents, "knowledge updates fail less often than previously reported", but a **parametric bias** remains: "the incorrect parametric answer appearing in context makes the knowledge update likelier to fail". — [arXiv 2404.16032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16032)
- **Tan, Sun, Yang, Wang, Cao, Cheng (2024), "Blinded by Generated Contexts: How Language Models Merge Generated and Retrieved Contexts When Knowledge Conflicts?", ACL 2024, arXiv:2401.11911 [V, bibliographic only].** — [arXiv 2401.11911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11911)
- **Ming, Purushwalkam, Pandit, Ke, Nguyen, Xiong, Joty (2024; ICLR 2025), "FaithEval: Can Your Language Model Stay Faithful to Context, Even If 'The Moon is Made of Marshmallows'", arXiv:2410.03727 [V-abs].** 4.9K problems (unanswerable / inconsistent / counterfactual contexts); "even state-of-the-art models often struggle to remain faithful to the given context, and … larger models do not necessarily exhibit improved faithfulness". — [arXiv 2410.03727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03727)
- **Su et al. (2024), "ConflictBank: A Benchmark for Evaluating the Influence of Knowledge Conflicts in LLM", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2408.12076 [V-abs]** — 7,453,853 claim–evidence pairs and 553,117 QA pairs. — [arXiv 2408.12076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12076)
- **Cheng, Pan, Yin, Wang, Wang (2024), "Understanding the Interplay between Parametric and Contextual Knowledge for Large Language Models" (EchoQA), arXiv:2410.08414 [V-abs].** The opposite failure: "LLMs tend to suppress their PK when contextual information is available, even when it is complementary or irrelevant", and "tailored instructions" only partly restore it. — [arXiv 2410.08414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08414)
- **Goyal, Baek, Kolter, Raghunathan (2024; ICLR 2025 Oral), "Context-Parametric Inversion: Why Instruction Finetuning Can Worsen Context Reliance", arXiv:2410.10796 [V-abs].** During instruction tuning, context reliance under conflict first rises then *falls* while benchmark scores keep rising (Llama, Mistral, Pythia; TULU, Alpaca, UltraChat). — [arXiv 2410.10796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10796)

**Mechanistic operationalizations**
- **Yu, Merullo, Pavlick (2023), "Characterizing Mechanisms for Factual Recall in Language Models", EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2310.15910 [V-abs].** World-capitals counterfactual prefix ("The capital of Poland is London"); the training frequency of *both* the query country and the in-context city "highly affect the models' likelihood of using the counterfactual" (Pythia, GPT-2); head attribution finds memory- vs. in-context-promoting heads; scaling a single head raises the in-context answer rate "to 88% of the time". — [arXiv 2310.15910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15910)
- **Geva, Bastings, Filippova, Globerson (2023), "Dissecting Recall of Factual Associations in Auto-Regressive Language Models", EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2304.14767 [V, bibliographic only]** — canonical account of subject-token enrichment and attribute extraction that later entity-level interventions target. — [arXiv 2304.14767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767)
- **Ortu, Jin, Doimo, Sachan, Cazzaniga, Schölkopf (2024), "Competition of Mechanisms: Tracing How Language Models Handle Facts and Counterfactuals", ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.11655 [V-abs]** — logit inspection and attention modification locate where factual and counterfactual mechanisms compete. — [arXiv 2402.11655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11655)
- **Jin, Cao, Yuan, Chen, Xu, Li, Jiang, Liu, Zhao (2024), "Cutting Off the Head Ends the Conflict: A Mechanism for Interpreting and Mitigating Knowledge Conflicts in Language Models", ACL 2024 [Findings status M], arXiv:2402.18154 [V-abs].** "Memory heads" vs. "context heads" in later layers; PH3 prunes conflicting heads without parameter updates, steering eight LMs toward internal memory (↑44.0%) or external context (↑38.5%). — [arXiv 2402.18154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18154)
- **Monea et al. (2023; ACL 2024), "A Glitch in the Matrix? Locating and Detecting Language Model Grounding with Fakepedia", arXiv:2312.02073 [V-abs]** — counterfactual texts built to clash with parametric knowledge; grounded vs. ungrounded answers are distinguishable "through computational analysis alone". — [arXiv 2312.02073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02073)
- **Minder, Du, Stoehr, Monea, Wendler, West, Cotterell (2024; ICLR 2025), "Controllable Context Sensitivity and the Knob Behind It", arXiv:2411.07404 [V-abs].** Task: same context+question, with an instruction to answer from context or from prior; fine-tuned Llama-3.1, Mistral-v0.3, Gemma-2 reach 85–95%; a **1-D subspace in a single layer** encodes context-vs-prior and transfers to non-fine-tuned instruct and base models. — [arXiv 2411.07404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07404)
- **Marjanović, Yu, Atanasova, Maistro, Lioma, Augenstein (2024), "DYNAMICQA: Tracing Internal Knowledge Conflicts in Language Models", Findings of EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2407.17023 [V, bibliographic only].** — [arXiv 2407.17023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17023)

### Inferences
- "Relied on memory" is always measured **differentially**: the same evidence is presented under two conditions that differ only in what the model could know from memory (original vs. substituted entity; with vs. without context; real vs. fictitious entity), and the output difference is attributed to the prior. For a 0–100 fraud score this translates into a *score-shift* statistic (score with real name − score with neutral/neighbor/swapped name on the identical filing) rather than a ratio of discrete answers.
- Xie et al.'s confirmation-bias result and Kortukov et al.'s "parametric answer appearing in context" result jointly imply that the worst case is not a clean conflict but a filing that contains prior-consistent fragments (e.g., a famous fraud firm's aggressive-growth language): the model will over-weight exactly those fragments.
- Because prior reliance scales with entity familiarity (Longpre popularity substitution, Du et al. susceptibility, Yu et al. frequency, Xie et al. popularity), bias should be largest for the most notorious/famous firms and near zero for obscure small caps — a testable dose-response prediction (bias vs. firm size/media coverage).
- Longpre-style naive substitution and Xie-style coherent rewriting give *opposite* conclusions about stubbornness; the coherence of the counterfactual document is itself a moderator, so name swaps in 10-Ks must be made consistently (name, ticker, products, executives) or the model may simply detect the edit.

### Gaps
- No knowledge-conflict paper found that studies **long regulatory documents** or **graded numeric judgments** (risk scores); all core benchmarks are short-answer QA/RE. Transfer of M_R-type results to 10-K scoring is an inference.
- Exact Table-level numbers for Xie et al. (order/quantity) and whether Jin et al. (PH3) and Li et al. (KAFT) are Findings vs. main-track papers were not confirmed against ACL Anthology pages.

## 2. Entity/name-driven bias in applied judgment tasks (finance, hiring, peer review, LLM-as-judge): what experimental designs are used to PROVE the bias?

### Takeaway
Applied audits prove entity/name bias with **correspondence-study logic**: hold the document constant, randomize only the identity cue (name present vs. absent; name A vs. name B; true vs. false attribution; blind vs. revealed), and test the outcome difference with paired statistics. In finance specifically, LLMs shift sentiment when a company name is merely added to identical text (Nakagawa et al. 2024), prefer large-cap/technology names under balanced evidence and resist counter-evidence (Lee et al. 2025), and their general knowledge of large firms *hurts* headline sentiment accuracy (Glasserman & Lin's "distraction effect"). I found no paper applying these designs to fraud-risk scoring of 10-K filings.

### Cited Findings

**Finance: firm-name, ticker, size and sector priors**
- **Nakagawa, Hirano, Fujimoto (2024), "Evaluating Company-specific Biases in Financial Sentiment Analysis using Large Language Models", IEEE BigData 2024 (special session), arXiv:2411.00420 [V].** Design: **name-present vs. name-absent prompt on identical text**; bias β = s_b − s_u on a 1–5 sentiment scale; Japanese earnings-summary texts (TDnet, Jan 2019–Dec 2023); seven models (GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-turbo, Gemini-1.5-pro/flash, Claude-3.5-sonnet, Claude-3-haiku, Qwen2-7B). Findings: "each LLM exhibits a bias in approximately 10-20% of cases"; direction is model-specific (most lean negative when the name is added; GPT-4o leans positive); GPT-3.5 tends to rate smaller companies more negatively when the name is included; bias groups show different subsequent cumulative abnormal returns (e.g., GPT-3.5 negative-bias group −1.15% at 60 days, p<.05). Limitations: Japanese market only; no mitigation tested; lenient significance thresholds; Qwen had many invalid outputs. (Table-level numbers were extracted by an automated reader of the arXiv HTML.) — [arXiv 2411.00420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00420)
- **Lee, Seo, Park, Lee, Ahn, Choi, Lopez-Lira, Lee (2025), "Your AI, Not Your View: The Bias of LLMs in Investment Analysis", ACM ICAIF 2025, arXiv:2507.20957 [V].** Design: frames the problem explicitly as a **knowledge conflict**; 427 S&P 500 stocks; synthetic buy/sell evidence each stating ±5% expected move; **balanced prompts** (equal buy/sell evidence, order randomized, repeated) give a latent preference score π = (N_buy − N_sell)/(N_buy + N_sell); **imbalanced prompts** (more or stronger counter-evidence) give a decision flip rate. Six models (GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-flash, Qwen3-235B, Mistral-Small-24B, Llama4-Scout, DeepSeek-V3). Findings: "a tendency to prefer technology stocks, large-cap stocks, and contrarian strategies"; sector and size differences significant at p<0.001 for all models; biases "often escalate into confirmation bias" — with mixed evidence, most models stay below a 60% flip rate even when counter-evidence is strengthened. Limitations: evidence generated by a single LLM; no mitigation tested; static snapshot. — [arXiv 2507.20957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20957)
- **Zhi, Zhang, Wang, Jiang, Ma, Guan, Shen (2025), "Exposing Product Bias in LLM Investment Recommendation", arXiv:2503.08750 [V-abs].** 567,000 samples over five asset classes; LLMs show "clear product preferences, such as certain stocks (e.g., 'AAPL' … and 'MSFT' …)"; "this bias persists even after applying debiasing techniques". — [arXiv 2503.08750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08750)
- **Dimino, Saxena, Sarmah, Pasquali (2025), "Uncovering Representation Bias for Investment Decisions in Open-Source Large Language Models", arXiv:2510.05702 [V-abs].** Balanced round-robin prompting over ~150 U.S. equities with constrained decoding and token-logit aggregation (Qwen models): "firm size and valuation consistently increase model confidence, while risk factors tend to decrease it". — [arXiv 2510.05702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05702)
- **Glasserman & Lin (2023), "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated By GPT Sentiment Analysis", arXiv:2309.17322 [V]; later in The Journal of Financial Data Science (2024) [S].** Design: **original vs. anonymized headline** pairs (company name, abbreviations and Knowledge-Graph-linked product names replaced with the string "Blahblahblah"); GPT-3.5; in-sample Jan 2015–Sep 2021, out-of-sample Oct 2021–Dec 2022. Defines the **distraction effect**: "general knowledge of the companies named interferes with the measurement of a text's sentiment". Findings: in-sample, anonymized headlines *outperform* (5.89 bp/day on scraped headlines, p = 0.066; 4.37 bp/day on Thomson Reuters headlines, p = 0.017), "particularly strong for larger companies"; out-of-sample, borderline evidence that anonymization still helps for large firms. Limitations: headlines only; no re-identification test of the anonymized headlines was found in the sections read; fuzzy-match masking. — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)
- Practitioner commentary (not peer-reviewed) argues LLMs "may systematically favor large, popular firms" because training-data attention, not fundamentals, drives salience. — [CFA Institute Enterprising Investor, 2026-02-18](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/investor/2026/02/18/attention-bias-in-ai-driven-investing/)

**Brand bias**
- **Kamruzzaman, Nguyen, Kim (2024), "'Global is Good, Local is Bad?': Understanding Brand Bias in LLMs", EMNLP 2024 (main), arXiv:2406.13997 [V-abs].** LLMs disproportionately associate global brands with positive attributes and recommend luxury gifts for people in high-income countries; country-of-origin effects. — [arXiv 2406.13997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13997)

**Name-substitution (correspondence) audits of demographic bias**
- **Salinas, Haim, Nyarko (2024), "What's in a Name? Auditing Large Language Models for Race and Gender Bias", arXiv:2402.14875 [V] (author order on the current arXiv record; the brief cited it as Haim, Salinas & Nyarko).** Design: advice prompts about a named individual (car purchase negotiation, election prediction, etc.), **42 prompt templates**, three context levels (Low / High / Numeric). Findings: advice "systematically disadvantages names that are commonly associated with racial minorities and women", Black-women names worst; "providing numerical, decision-relevant anchors in the prompt can successfully counteract the biases, qualitative details have inconsistent effects and may even increase disparities". Limitation: names proxy perceived race/gender; arXiv only. — [arXiv 2402.14875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14875)
- **Tamkin, Askell, Lovitt, Durmus, Joseph, Kravec, Nguyen, Kaplan, Ganguli (2023), "Evaluating and Mitigating Discrimination in Language Model Decisions", arXiv:2312.03689 [V].** Design: LM-generated decision templates over **70 decision scenarios**; demographics varied either *explicitly* or *implicitly via names*; mixed-effects discrimination score on logit p(yes). Findings (Claude 2.0): positive discrimination for non-male / non-white, negative for age >60; effects larger when demographics are explicit than when inferred from names. Mitigation results in §4. - **An, Huang, Lin, Tai (2024), "Measuring Gender and Racial Biases in Large Language Models", arXiv:2403.15281 [V-abs].** ~361,000 resumes with randomized identities scored by GPT: higher scores for female candidates, lower for Black male candidates; "1 or 2 percentage point difference in hiring probabilities". — [arXiv 2403.15281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15281)

**Design catalogue distilled from the above (each traced to a source)**
- *Presence/absence ablation* (name in prompt vs. not): Nakagawa et al.; Glasserman & Lin; Howell et al. (blind vs. non-blind). — [arXiv 2411.00420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00420)
- *Name swap / counterfactual substitution* on a fixed document: Longpre et al.; Yan et al.; all hiring audits; framed as Perturbation Sensitivity Analysis by **Prabhakaran, Hutchinson, Mitchell (2019), "Perturbation Sensitivity Analysis to Detect Unintended Model Biases", EMNLP 2019, arXiv:1910.04210 [V-abs]** ("I hate Katy Perry" should score the same as "I hate Taylor Swift"). — [arXiv 1910.04210](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04210)
- *False attribution / label swap*: Saraf et al.; Chae et al. — [arXiv 2508.21164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21164)
- *Placebo or fictitious entities / arbitrary strings*: Yan et al. (arbitrary strings); Glasserman & Lin ("Blahblahblah"); Monea et al. (Fakepedia). — [arXiv 2110.08555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08555)
- *Balanced-evidence latent-preference elicitation and flip-rate under imbalanced evidence*: Lee et al. — [arXiv 2507.20957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20957)
- *Dose-response on familiarity* (popularity buckets, firm size terciles): Longpre et al.; Glasserman & Lin; Lopez-Lira et al. (§5). — [arXiv 2109.05052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05052)
- *Relative (pairwise) rather than absolute judgments* to expose implicit bias: Bai et al. — [arXiv 2402.04105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04105)
- *Many templates + mixed-effects models* to show the effect is systematic rather than prompt-idiosyncratic: Salinas et al. (42 templates); Tamkin et al. (70 scenarios); counter-example of prompt sensitivity in An et al. (ACL 2024). — [arXiv 2402.14875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14875)
- *Token-level soft ratings (logit-based scores)* reveal bias hidden in discretized outputs: Macharla Vasu et al.; Dimino et al. — [arXiv 2509.13400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13400)

### Inferences
- The project's blinded round with matched industry pairs corresponds to the *presence/absence ablation*. The literature suggests two additional arms that isolate the name effect more sharply than blinding alone: (a) a **cross-swap arm** (fraud firm's filing under a clean firm's name and vice versa, coherently edited), and (b) a **name-only arm** (ask for the 0–100 score given only the company name/year, no filing) that directly estimates the prior the model brings — analogous to Nakagawa's β and to the "date-only recall query" used by Gao, Jiang & Yan for lookahead propensity ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)).
- Salinas et al.'s "numeric anchor" result and Lee et al.'s confirmation-bias result point the same way: identity priors dominate when evidence is qualitative/ambiguous and shrink when decisive quantitative evidence is supplied. For 10-Ks, structured numeric red-flag inputs (ratios, accrual measures) should reduce name-driven variance relative to free-text narrative.
- Peer-review and LLM-judge studies show that the *direction* of prestige bias is toward leniency for prestigious entities — the same sign as the hypothesised "Microsoft scores low" effect.

### Gaps
- **I found no reliable source that tests entity/name bias in LLM fraud detection or risk scoring of 10-K filings** (arXiv API queries combining company name / fraud / 10-K / memorization returned nothing relevant). The closest evidence is sentiment (Nakagawa; Glasserman & Lin) and investment recommendation (Lee; Zhi; Dimino).
- Bowen et al. and the Bloomberg audit were not opened at the primary source (SSRN / Bloomberg paywall); details above come from index pages and secondary summaries.
- A claimed NBER result that LLM-built portfolios concentrate in ~10 stocks surfaced only via a low-quality news site and could not be traced to a primary paper; it is not used here.

## 3. Stereotype-bias measurement foundations (brief)

### Takeaway
Classic stereotype benchmarks measure association preference (WEAT, StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs) or whether stereotypes override an informative context (BBQ); they are useful as conceptual ancestors of "prior vs. evidence" tests but have documented validity problems, and the applied literature has largely moved to counterfactual decision audits (§2).

### Cited Findings
- **Caliskan, Bryson, Narayanan (2017), "Semantics derived automatically from language corpora contain human-like biases", Science 356, arXiv:1608.07187 [V].** WEAT: an Implicit-Association-Test analogue on GloVe embeddings; replicates human IAT biases. — [arXiv 1608.07187](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07187)
- **Nadeem, Bethke, Reddy (2020; ACL 2021), "StereoSet: Measuring stereotypical bias in pretrained language models", arXiv:2004.09456 [V].** Stereotype preference in four domains (gender, profession, race, religion). — [arXiv 2004.09456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09456)
- **Nangia, Vania, Bhalerao, Bowman (2020), "CrowS-Pairs: A Challenge Dataset for Measuring Social Biases in Masked Language Models", EMNLP 2020, arXiv:2010.00133 [V].** 1,508 minimally different sentence pairs, nine bias types; all three evaluated MLMs favour the stereotyping sentence in every category. — [arXiv 2010.00133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00133)
- **Parrish, Chen, Nangia, Padmakumar, Phang, Thompson, Htut, Bowman (2021; Findings of ACL 2022), "BBQ: A Hand-Built Bias Benchmark for Question Answering", arXiv:2110.08193 [V].** Nine social dimensions; two levels — under-informative context (does the answer reflect the stereotype?) and adequately informative context (does the bias *override* the correct answer?); models are "up to 3.4 percentage points" more accurate when the correct answer aligns with a social bias. BBQ's disambiguated-context condition is structurally the same test as a knowledge conflict. — [arXiv 2110.08193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08193)
- **Gallegos, Rossi, Barrow, Tanjim, Kim, Dernoncourt, Yu, Zhang, Ahmed (2023), "Bias and Fairness in Large Language Models: A Survey", Computational Linguistics 50(3), 2024, arXiv:2309.00770 [V].** Taxonomies of metrics (embedding-, probability-, generated-text-based), datasets, and mitigation (pre-, in-, intra-, post-processing). — [arXiv 2309.00770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00770)
- **Blodgett, Lopez, Olteanu, Sim, Wallach (2021), "Stereotyping Norwegian Salmon: An Inventory of Pitfalls in Fairness Benchmark Datasets", ACL-IJCNLP 2021, pp. 1004–1015 [V via ACL Anthology].** Measurement-modelling critique of four benchmarks (incl. StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs): they "frequently lack clear articulations of what is being measured" and contain pairs that do not validly operationalize stereotyping. — [ACL Anthology 2021.acl-long.81](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.81)
- **Gonen & Goldberg (2019), "Lipstick on a Pig: Debiasing Methods Cover up Systematic Gender Biases in Word Embeddings But do not Remove Them", NAACL 2019, arXiv:1903.03862 [V].** After debiasing, "the actual effect is mostly hiding the bias, not removing it"; bias is recoverable from neighbourhood structure. — [arXiv 1903.03862](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03862)

### Inferences
- The Blodgett et al. lesson — state precisely what construct is being measured — applies to the project: "entity bias" should be defined as a causal contrast (score change attributable to identity knowledge at fixed evidence), not as a correlation between scores and reputation, since reputation legitimately correlates with true fraud risk.

### Gaps
- Dataset sizes/metric details for StereoSet (ICAT score, test-set size) were not re-read in this session; only abstract-level facts are given.

## 4. Mitigation without retraining: masking, counterfactual substitution, prompting, self-debiasing, causal intervention, concept erasure / activation steering, blinded protocols — how well do they work and where do they fail?

### Takeaway
Every inference-time family works in controlled settings and has a documented failure mode: **masking** removes the cue but leaks through context and destroys useful information; **"ignore/blind" instructions** work on simple templated decisions but fail against memorized facts, in realistic contexts, and under mixed evidence; **neighbor-entity / counterfactual substitution** preserves more information than masking but is validated only on short QA/RE; **decoding-level contrast (CAD)** needs logits and over-corrects; **activation-level interventions** (direction ablation / affine editing, head pruning, SAE features) give the most robust results in recent hiring-bias work but need white-box access, can have off-target effects, and are weaker on realistic tasks in several 2025–2026 studies.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Entity masking / anonymization / placeholder substitution**
- Masking as a causal cut: Wang et al. (2023) note that entity masking (Zhang et al. 2017) follows the principle of cutting the causal link between specific entities and labels, but a placeholder without definition collapses RE F1 from 71.8 to 37.9. — [arXiv 2305.14695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14695)
- Masking "degrades the RE performance because it loses the semantic information of entities" (Wang et al., CoRE, NAACL 2022). — [arXiv 2205.03784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03784)
- **Engelberg, Manela, Mullins, Vulicevic (2025), "Entity Neutering", SSRN 5182756 [S — citation confirmed from the reference lists of two arXiv papers; abstract seen only as reproduced on a seminar listing].** Method: an LLM agent iteratively **masks entity-related terms and paraphrases** until an *independent* LLM fails to recognize the company. Reported results: on >500,000 financial news articles, after neutering "ChatGPT and other LLMs identify the subject firm at the rate of random chance"; for unidentified articles, raw vs. neutered sentiment agree >90% of the time (the difference bounds look-ahead bias); also applied to MD&As and earnings-call transcripts. — [citation in arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364), [citation and adapted prompts in arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)
- **Wu, Yang, Ying, Zhou (2025), "Anonymization and Information Loss", arXiv:2511.15364 [V].** Compares NER placeholder masking (spaCy `en_core_web_trf`, e.g., "FIRM_1") against the Engelberg-style iterative LLM method (GPT-4o-mini, up to three rounds). Findings: residual firm-name identification 66.88% (NER) vs. 9.04% (LLM iterative); anonymized texts lose predictive signal — in horse-race regressions for S&P credit downgrades the raw-text coefficient stays significant (0.073, t=3.58) while anonymized ones go to ≈0; ~59% of the R² decline is attributed to "interpretive distortion" rather than removed evidence; masking NUMBERS and AGENTS entities costs the most; loss is larger for bigger models; introduces an "anonymization gap" metric requiring no outcome data; "Look-ahead bias does not explain this difference". Limitation: the paper's concern is look-ahead bias, not reputation bias; numbers extracted by an automated reader of the arXiv HTML. — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364)
- **Sheikhi, Huang, Zaïane (2025), "Improving LLM's Attachment to External Knowledge In Dialogue Generation Tasks Through Entity Anonymization", arXiv:2511.11946 [V-abs]** — LLMs "often rely on internal knowledge … even when they are given a flawlessly retrieved knowledge graph"; entity anonymization improves attachment to the external KG (OpenDialKG). — [arXiv 2511.11946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11946)
- **Jeon & Lee (2026), "Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade? An Anonymization-First Framework for Portfolio Optimization", ICLR 2026 Workshop on Advances in Financial AI, arXiv:2603.17692 [V].** Replaces tickers, company, subsidiary and product names with synthetic IDs ("AAPL" → "STOCK 0026"); validates with **negative-control experiments (random shuffling)**. Stated limitation: "Anonymization effect is not directly validated" (no raw-vs-anonymized ablation), and the authors do "not claim this blocks all leakage". — [arXiv 2603.17692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17692)
- **Garg, Perot, Limtiaco, Taly, Chi, Beutel (2018; AIES 2019), "Counterfactual Fairness in Text Classification through Robustness", arXiv:1809.10610 [V-abs]** — "blindness" (token replacement), counterfactual augmentation and counterfactual logit pairing; blindness and CLP address counterfactual token fairness without harming classifier performance (training-time, small classifiers). — [arXiv 1809.10610](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10610)

**(b) Counterfactual substitution, augmentation and invariance tests**
- Neighbor-entity in-context intervention (Wang et al. 2023): up to +20.5 EM (MRC) and −17.6 M_R points (RE) on GPT-3.5 — see §1. — [arXiv 2305.14695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14695)
- **Veitch, D'Amour, Yadlowsky, Eisenstein (2021), "Counterfactual Invariance to Spurious Correlations: Why and How to Pass Stress Tests", NeurIPS 2021 (spotlight), arXiv:2106.00545 [V-abs]** — formalizes "changing irrelevant parts of the input shouldn't change model predictions"; the right regularizer depends on the causal structure of the data. — [arXiv 2106.00545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00545)
- **Ribeiro, Wu, Guestrin, Singh (2020), "Beyond Accuracy: Behavioral Testing of NLP models with CheckList", ACL 2020, arXiv:2005.04118 [V, bibliographic]**; **Kaushik, Hovy, Lipton (2019; ICLR 2020), "Learning the Difference that Makes a Difference with Counterfactually-Augmented Data", arXiv:1909.12434 [V, bibliographic]**; **Kusner, Loftus, Russell, Silva (2017), "Counterfactual Fairness", arXiv:1703.06856 [V, bibliographic; NeurIPS 2017 M]** — the invariance-test and counterfactual-fairness foundations. — [arXiv 2005.04118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04118), [arXiv 1909.12434](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12434), [arXiv 1703.06856](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06856)
- Counterfactual *demonstrations* in the prompt improve context faithfulness, while factual demonstrations can impair it (Zhou et al. 2023). — [arXiv 2303.11315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11315)
- **Li, Zeng, Xing, Xu, Xu (2025), "Profit Mirage: Revisiting Information Leakage in LLM-based Financial Agents", arXiv:2510.07920 [V-abs]** — FactFin applies "counterfactual perturbations to compel LLM-based agents to learn causal drivers instead of memorized outcomes". — [arXiv 2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920)

**(c) Instruction-based debiasing and "blind"/"ignore prior knowledge" prompts — evidence FOR**
- **Tamkin et al. (2023) [V].** Interventions appended to the prompt include "Really (4x) don't discriminate", "Illegal to discriminate", "Ignore demographics" (the profile is shown only because of "a technical quirk"; decide "as though no protected characteristics had been revealed" and imagine a redacted profile), "Illegal + Ignore", "Don't use affirmative action", and "Precog" think-aloud variants. "Illegal to discriminate" and "Ignore demographics" achieve "low discrimination score (≈ 0.15) and high correlations with the Default predictions (≈ 92%)". — [arXiv 2312.03689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03689)
- **Ganguli et al. (2023), "The Capacity for Moral Self-Correction in Large Language Models", arXiv:2302.07459 [V-abs]** — the capability to follow instructions to avoid biased outputs "emerges at 22B model parameters" and improves with size and RLHF. — [arXiv 2302.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07459)
- **Si, Gan, Yang, Wang, Wang, Boyd-Graber, Wang (2022; ICLR 2023), "Prompting GPT-3 To Be Reliable", arXiv:2210.09150 [V-abs]** — natural-language instructions reduce social biases; prompts can update the LLM's factual knowledge. — [arXiv 2210.09150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09150)
- **Gallegos et al. (2024), "Self-Debiasing Large Language Models: Zero-Shot Recognition and Reduction of Stereotypes", arXiv:2402.01981 [V-abs]** — self-debiasing via explanation and via reprompting reduces stereotyping across nine social groups; reprompting gives the largest reduction. — [arXiv 2402.01981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01981)
- **Schick, Udupa, Schütze (2021), "Self-Diagnosis and Self-Debiasing: A Proposal for Reducing Corpus-Based Bias in NLP", TACL 9 (2021), arXiv:2103.00453 [V].** A *decoding* algorithm: given only a textual description of the undesired behaviour, it lowers the probability of text the model itself diagnoses as biased; no training or word lists. Authors: "we by no means eliminate the issue". Requires access to token probabilities. — [arXiv 2103.00453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00453)
- **Furniturewala et al. (2024), "'Thinking' Fair and Slow: On the Efficacy of Structured Prompts for Debiasing Language Models", EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2405.10431 [V-abs]** — System-2 style "Implicative Prompts" give lower mean bias than simpler instruction/role prompts. — [arXiv 2405.10431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10431)
- **Echterhoff, Liu, Alessa, McAuley, He (2024), "Cognitive Bias in Decision-Making with LLMs", Findings of EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2403.00811 [V-abs]** — BiasBuster (13,465 prompts); "self-help" debiasing in which the LLM rewrites its own prompt to remove bias-inducing content. — [arXiv 2403.00811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00811)
- Bowen et al. [S]: an instruction to decide without bias removed the mortgage approval gap. — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arz/wpaper/eres2025_75.html)
- Zhou et al. (2023): instruction-based prompts beat the base prompt, but opinion-based reframing beats plain instructions. — [arXiv 2303.11315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11315)
- **Wu et al. (2025), "AxBench: Steering LLMs? Even Simple Baselines Outperform Sparse Autoencoders", ICML 2025, arXiv:2501.17148 [V-abs]** — for *steering toward concepts* on Gemma-2-2B/9B, "prompting outperforms all existing methods, followed by finetuning"; SAEs are not competitive. — [arXiv 2501.17148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148)

**(c') Instruction-based debiasing — evidence AGAINST**
- **Lopez-Lira, Tang, Zhu (2025), "The Memorization Problem: Can We Trust LLMs' Economic Forecasts?", arXiv:2504.14765 [V].** "Instructions to respect historical boundaries fail to prevent recall-level accuracy": with a fake December-2010 cutoff given only in the system prompt, GPT-4o's quarterly-GDP directional accuracy is 97.59% pre- and 98.00% post-fake-cutoff; with a rolling "Do not use any knowledge after time t−1" instruction on S&P 500 levels the pre/post pattern is unchanged. Authors' recommendation: do not treat knowledge-restricting prompts as a fix. — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)
- **Karvonen & Marks (2025), "Robustly Improving LLM Fairness in Realistic Settings via Interpretability", arXiv:2506.10922 [V].** Four anti-bias prompts of increasing strength keep bias near zero in the simple (Tamkin-style) setting, but once realistic context is added — **company names (Meta, General Motors, Palantir)**, culture text from careers pages, and a selectivity constraint ("only accept candidates in the top 10%") — bias reappears "up to 12% differences in interview rates" across GPT-4o, Claude 4 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemma-2 27B, Gemma-3, Mistral-24B (e.g., Mistral Small 24B from 2% to 11%). Models also become biased from indirect cues (college affiliation), and the bias is **invisible in chain-of-thought**: no reasoning mentioning race/gender was found even in cases where the decision flipped. — [arXiv 2506.10922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10922)
- Zhi et al. (2025): product bias in investment recommendation "persists even after applying debiasing techniques". — [arXiv 2503.08750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08750)
- Lee et al. (2025): with mixed evidence, models "cling to initial judgments even when faced with increasing counter-evidence". — [arXiv 2507.20957](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20957)
- Bai et al. (2024): models that pass explicit bias tests still show implicit bias in relative decisions. — [arXiv 2402.04105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04105)
- Macharla Vasu et al. (2025): "alignment may mask but not fully eliminate underlying preferences" (visible in soft ratings). — [arXiv 2509.13400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13400)
- Goyal et al. (2024): instruction tuning itself can *reduce* context reliance under conflict. — [arXiv 2410.10796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10796)
- **Tripathy & Buckmann (2026), "Fair outputs, Biased Internals: Causal Potency and Asymmetry of Latent Bias in LLMs for High-Stakes Decisions", arXiv:2605.15217 [V-abs]** — in mortgage underwriting with matched applications differing only in racially associated names, open-weight models "show no output-level bias, yet retain and amplify demographic representations across model layers"; reinjecting them by steering produces "near-complete decision reversals"; "behavioural audits focused on outputs are insufficient". — [arXiv 2605.15217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15217)

**(e) Concept erasure in representations**
- **Ravfogel, Elazar, Gonen, Twiton, Goldberg (2020), "Null It Out: Guarding Protected Attributes by Iterative Nullspace Projection", ACL 2020, arXiv:2004.07667 [V-abs]** — INLP: repeatedly train a linear probe and project onto its nullspace. — [arXiv 2004.07667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07667)
- **Ravfogel, Twiton, Goldberg, Cotterell (2022), "Linear Adversarial Concept Erasure", ICML 2022, arXiv:2201.12091 [V-abs]** — R-LACE: minimax formulation; existing solutions "generally not optimal". — [arXiv 2201.12091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12091)

- **Zou et al. - **Tan, Chanin, Lynch, Kanoulas, Paige, Garriga-Alonso, Kirk (2024), "Analyzing the Generalization and Reliability of Steering Vectors", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2407.12404 [V-abs]** — "steerability is highly variable across different inputs"; for several concepts steering vectors are "brittle to reasonable changes in the prompt". — [arXiv 2407.12404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12404)
- Context-vs-prior steering specifically: Yu et al. (single-head scaling, 88%); Jin et al. PH3 (head pruning); Minder et al. (1-D subspace); **Zhao et al. (2024; NAACL 2025), "Steering Knowledge Selection Behaviours in LLMs via SAE-Based Representation Engineering" (SpARE), arXiv:2410.15999 [V-abs]** — training-free SAE-feature editing that controls which knowledge source is used, "surpassing existing representation engineering methods (+10%) as well as contrastive decoding methods (+15%)"; **Li, Chen, Tong (2025), "Taming Knowledge Conflicts in Language Models", ICML 2025 (spotlight), arXiv:2503.10996 [V-abs]** — shows "superposition of contextual information and parametric memory" in the same heads (so pure memory-head/context-head pruning is an oversimplification) and proposes JuICE, a dual-run test-time attention intervention evaluated on 11 datasets and 6 architectures; **Shi et al. **(g) SAE-based feature ablation / steering (2024–2026)**
- **Marks, Rager, Michaud, Belinkov, Bau, Mueller (2024; ICLR 2025), "Sparse Feature Circuits: Discovering and Editing Interpretable Causal Graphs in Language Models", arXiv:2403.19647 [V-abs]** — SHIFT: improve a classifier's generalization "by ablating features that a human judges to be task-irrelevant". — [arXiv 2403.19647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19647)
- **Durmus, Tamkin, Clark, Wei, Marcus, Batson, Handa, Lovitt, Tong, McCain, Rausch, Huang, Bowman, Ritchie, Henighan, Ganguli (Anthropic, 25 Oct 2024), "Evaluating feature steering: A case study in mitigating social biases" [V via the publisher page].** 29 bias-related SAE features in Claude 3 Sonnet, evaluated on BBQ, MMLU, PubMedQA: a steering "sweet spot" (factors −5 to 5) without significant capability loss; **off-target effects** (a gender-bias-awareness feature also raised age-bias scores by 13%); a "Multiple perspectives" feature reduced overall BBQ bias by ~3%; preliminary appendix result that prompt engineering had "comparable" sensitivity/specificity profiles. — [Anthropic research post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/evaluating-feature-steering)
- **Ferrando, Obeso, Rajamanoharan, Nanda (2024; ICLR 2025), "Do I Know This Entity? Directly relevant as a possible "make the model treat Enron as unknown" lever (not tested for that purpose). — [arXiv 2411.14257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14257)
- **Yona, Barzilay, Karasik, Geva (2026), "Friends and Grandmothers in Silico: Localizing Entity Cells in Language Models", arXiv:2604.01404 [V-abs].** Sparse entity-selective MLP neurons in early layers; in Qwen2.5-7B "suppressing a localized cell selectively erases recall for its matched entity while leaving others intact"; the same cells respond to aliases, acronyms, misspellings and multilingual forms; causal signal varies across model families. — [arXiv 2604.01404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01404)
- **Shan & Mueller (2025), "Measuring Mechanistic Independence: Can Bias Be Removed Without Erasing Demographics?", EACL 2026 (per Semantic Scholar), arXiv:2512.20796 [V-abs]** — targeted SAE feature ablations in Gemma-2-9B reduce profession stereotypes while preserving name-based demographic recognition, but in education tasks removing attribution features "induces 'prior collapse', thus increasing overall bias". — [arXiv 2512.20796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20796)

- **Zhang, Sun, Han, Xu (2026), "Key Path Identification for Resolving Knowledge Conflicts via SAE-based Steering", arXiv:2609.08173 [V-abs]** — mass steering of many correlated SAE features adds noise; steering a small causally selected subset improves accuracy by 18% on average over the best mass-steering baseline. — [arXiv 2609.08173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08173)
- AxBench (ICML 2025): SAEs not competitive with prompting or simple difference-in-means for steering/detection. — [arXiv 2501.17148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148)

### Inferences
- For a **closed-API** model (no logits, no activations) the feasible inference-time toolbox is input-side only: masking/neutering, neighbor-entity placeholders, opinion-style reframing, counterfactual demonstrations, structured prompts, and multi-agent anonymize→verify→score pipelines. CAD, self-debiasing decoding, LEACE-style erasure, CAA/affine editing, head pruning and SAE interventions all require open weights or at least logits.
- The evidence on "ignore prior knowledge" instructions is consistent once stratified by what must be ignored: instructions suppress use of an **explicit, discrete attribute** in simple decisions (Tamkin; Bowen), but do not suppress **memorized facts** (Lopez-Lira) nor attribute use in **realistic, cue-rich contexts** (Karvonen & Marks). Reputation knowledge about a named firm is of the second kind, so an instruction alone should be assumed insufficient and at best a supplementary layer.
- Wang et al.'s neighbor-entity definition is the only black-box technique explicitly designed to keep the *useful* part of entity information (type/industry/scale) while diluting the specific prior — the property the Wu et al. information-loss result says naive masking lacks. A natural adaptation is "Assume [COMPANY] can be any of {real firm + k matched-industry/size peers}"; this is an untested extrapolation.
- Chain-of-thought inspection is not a valid check that the model ignored identity (Karvonen & Marks; Tripathy & Buckmann): bias can operate without being verbalized, and outputs can look fair while internals are not.
- White-box results suggest a concrete research option on open models: locate the "known-entity" / entity-cell signal (Ferrando et al.; Yona et al.) or a reputation direction (difference of means between notorious and neutral firms, à la Karvonen & Marks) and apply affine editing; no paper has done this for firm reputation.

### Gaps
- No study found that compares these families head-to-head on the *same* entity-bias task with a graded-score output; CUB (§6) is the closest but covers QA/fact-checking context utilisation.
- Entity Neutering's own numbers were not read from the SSRN paper (network access to the seminar page was blocked; SSRN not opened).
- No evidence found on whether opinion-based reframing or counterfactual demonstrations (Zhou et al.) transfer to long documents or to 2025–2026 frontier models.
- Whether concept-erasure/steering can remove a *specific entity's* reputation (rather than a binary attribute) without collateral damage is untested in the sources found.

## 5. Evidence that LLMs can identify masked entities from context (masking is leaky), and best practices for testing whether blinding worked

### Takeaway
Masking names is not blinding: GPT-4o re-identified the firm in 100% of entity-neutered Apple/Meta/Microsoft earnings calls and in 66% of large-cap calls (Lopez-Lira et al. 2025), and NER-style placeholder masking left 67% of firms identifiable (Wu et al. 2025); re-identification rises with firm prominence, document length, model size and background knowledge. The emerging best practice is an explicit **manipulation check** — ask the same (or a stronger) model to guess the entity and period from the blinded text, report the hit rate against a chance baseline, iterate masking/paraphrasing until it fails, and treat a passed check as a lower bound on leakage rather than proof of blindness.

### Cited Findings

**Leakage evidence in finance**
- **Lopez-Lira, Tang, Zhu (2025), arXiv:2504.14765 [V].** "masking fails as LLMs reconstruct entities and dates from minimal context". Entity-neutered (adapted from Engelberg et al.) earnings-call transcripts, Jan 2006–Sep 2023, GPT-4o: **firm accuracy** AAPL 100%, META 100%, MSFT 100%, GOOGL 91.89%, NVDA 93.55%, TSLA 85.71%, AMZN 85.33%; by NYSE size tercile Large 65.77%, Medium 40.78%, Small 30.90%; AAPL year accuracy 95.95% and quarter-and-year accuracy 93.24%. Even a one-sentence anonymized "economic logic" derived from a headline lets GPT-4o identify the firm 11.9%–46.3% of the time for five of the Magnificent Seven (3.3% GOOGL, 0.6% NVDA). Identification prompt: "You will receive a body of text which has been anonymized. You are omniscient. Use all your knowledge and the context to identify which company and industry the text is about, as well as the quarter and year it was written. Make your best guess…". Interpretation rule stated by the authors: successful reconstruction proves the masked task is not invariant to memorized knowledge; **failed reconstruction does not prove invariance**. — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)
- **Wu, Yang, Ying, Zhou (2025), arXiv:2511.15364 [V].** Residual identification after masking: NER placeholders — firm names 66.88%, tickers 53.90%, dates 6.03%; iterative LLM mask-and-paraphrase (up to 3 rounds) — firm names 9.04%, dates 2.81%. Procedure: (1) masking, (2) re-identification test, (3) conditional paraphrasing if the test fails, (4) second re-identification test, escalating to more aggressive prompts. — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364)
- **Engelberg et al. (2025) [S]:** after iterative neutering, LLMs identify the subject firm of news articles at the rate of random chance. Apparent conflict with Lopez-Lira et al., who report high re-identification on transcripts "anonymized using the entity neutering approach of Engelberg et al." — plausibly due to document type/length (short news vs. long earnings calls), single-pass vs. iterate-until-unidentified implementation, and choice of adversary model; neither paper's primary text resolves this. — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765); contrasted with the Engelberg et al. claim as cited in [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364)
- **Kim, Muhn, Nikolaev (2024), "Financial Statement Analysis with Large Language Models", arXiv:2407.17866 [V; v3 of 20 Feb 2025 is a withdrawal].** Manipulation check on standardized, anonymized *numeric* statements (names omitted, years replaced by t, t−1): GPT asked to guess the company name and fiscal year "correctly identifies the firm name with an accuracy of 0.07%" and the year with 2.95%. They contrast this with **Sarkar & Vafa (2024), "Lookahead Bias in Pretrained Language Models", SSRN 4754678 [S]**, whose guessing test used anonymous *textual* disclosures. **Caution:** the arXiv comment states the working paper was temporarily withdrawn because "a co-author identified inconsistencies in the data and analyses"; cite only for the design of the check, not for results. — [arXiv 2407.17866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17866)
- Glasserman & Lin (2023) recognised the leak channel without measuring it: a replaced headline such as "Blahblahblah announces release of new iPhone" is still attributable, hence their additional product-name replacement via the Google Knowledge Graph. — [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)
- **Gao, Jiang, Yan (2025), "Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts", arXiv:2512.23847 [V-abs]** — a complementary diagnostic rather than a masking test: a recall-only query for a firm–date pair yields a "Lookahead Propensity" that is positive in-sample and collapses to ~0 after the training cutoff; an interaction between LAP and forecast accuracy flags contamination. (Look-ahead topic covered by other researchers; listed for the *name-only probe* design.) — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)

**Leakage evidence in privacy / de-identification research**
- **Staab, Vero, Balunović, Vechev (2023), "Beyond Memorization: Violating Privacy Via Inference with Large Language Models", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.07298 [V].** LLMs infer personal attributes from Reddit text with "up to 85% top-1 and 95% top-3 accuracy". - **Patsakis & Lykousas (2023), "Man vs the machine: The Struggle for Effective Text Anonymisation in the Age of Large Language Models", arXiv:2303.12429 [V-abs; journal publication in Scientific Reports M]** — GPT de-anonymizes anonymised texts about famous people; proposes LLM-assisted anonymisation. — [arXiv 2303.12429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12429)
- **Nyffenegger, Stürmer, Niklaus (2023; Findings of NAACL 2024), "Anonymity at Risk? - **Charpentier & Lison (2025), "Re-identification of De-identified Documents with Autoregressive Infilling", ACL 2025, arXiv:2505.12859 [V-abs]** — retrieval + infilling attack recovers "as many as 80% of de-identified text spans"; accuracy rises with background knowledge. - **Huang, Chen, Shu (2024), "Can Large Language Models Identify Authorship?", Findings of EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2403.08213 [V, bibliographic only].** — [arXiv 2403.08213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08213)
- LLM-based maskers themselves miss entities: **Pham, Kairouz, Mireshghallah, Bagdasarian, Pham, Houmansadr (2025), "Can Large Language Models Really Recognize Your Name?", ACM FAccT '26, arXiv:2505.14549 [V-abs]** — recall of ambiguous names drops 20–40% across 12 LLMs. — [arXiv 2505.14549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14549)

**How blinding success is assessed in other fields**
- **Bang, Ni, Davis (2004), "Assessment of blinding in clinical trials", Controlled Clinical Trials 25(2):143–156 [S].** Blinding index per arm scaled −1 to 1: "1 being complete lack of blinding, 0 being consistent with perfect blinding and −1 indicating opposite guessing"; James et al.'s (1996, Statistics in Medicine) index weights "don't know" answers; reviews recommend reporting both. — [tutorial comparing blinding indices (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12547027/), [review of blinding assessment (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19758415/)
- **Le Goues, Brun, Apel, Berger, Khurshid, Smaragdakis (2018), "Effectiveness of Anonymization in Double-Blind Review", Communications of the ACM 61(6):34–37, arXiv:1709.01609 [V].** Reviewers at ASE, OOPSLA and PLDI 2016 were asked to guess authors: "74%-90% of reviews contain no correct guess"; self-identified experts guess more often but not more accurately. — [arXiv 1709.01609](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01609)

**Manipulation-check practices that appear in the literature**
- Ask the scoring model (and ideally a stronger "omniscient" adversary) to guess firm, industry and period; report accuracy by firm-prominence stratum (Lopez-Lira et al.; Wu et al.; Kim et al.). — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)
- Use the re-identification test as a *gate* inside the anonymization loop and escalate masking/paraphrasing until it fails (Engelberg et al. [S]; Wu et al.; Staab et al. 2025 adversarial anonymization). — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364), [arXiv 2402.13846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13846)
- Quantify the information cost of blinding (raw-vs-anonymized agreement >90% in Engelberg et al. [S]; "anonymization gap" in Wu et al.). — [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364)
- Negative controls (shuffled inputs) to show the remaining signal is real (Jeon & Lee 2026). — [arXiv 2603.17692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17692)
- Post-training-cutoff or unknown-entity samples as the cleanest validation (Lopez-Lira et al. recommend making post-cutoff data the primary analysis). — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765)

### Inferences
- A 10-K is close to the worst case for masking: it is long, and contains business descriptions, product and segment names, executive and auditor names, headquarters, exact financial magnitudes and fiscal dates — all of the context types that drive re-identification in the earnings-call and court-ruling studies. Name-only masking should be assumed leaky for well-known registrants; Lopez-Lira's size-tercile gradient (66% → 31%) suggests leakage is concentrated exactly where reputation priors are strongest.
- A defensible manipulation check for the project would report, per filing: (i) top-1/top-k firm-guess accuracy and year-guess accuracy from the blinded text vs. a chance baseline; (ii) a Bang-style index computed on forced guesses (e.g., "fraud firm vs. matched control"), where 0 indicates successful blinding; (iii) the analysis re-run on the subset of filings the model could *not* identify; and (iv) whether score differences between matched pairs shrink in that subset. The combination is my synthesis; no single source prescribes it.
- Because failed guessing is only a lower bound on leakage (Lopez-Lira et al.), and because recognition can be implicit (Tripathy & Buckmann; Karvonen & Marks' unverbalized bias), the guess test should be complemented by behavioural invariance tests (name swaps, §2) rather than relied on alone.
- Heavier neutering (numbers, agents, dates) lowers identifiability but removes the entity types Wu et al. find most informative; for fraud detection, numbers are likely the core evidence, so the masking/identifiability trade-off must be measured, not assumed.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures LLM re-identification of **masked 10-K / annual-report text** specifically (evidence is for earnings calls, news headlines, MD&A as claimed by Engelberg et al. [S], court rulings, Wikipedia, Reddit).
- No source found that applies a formal blinding index (Bang/James) to LLM evaluation; that transfer is my suggestion.
- The Sarkar & Vafa guessing-test result is known only via Kim et al.'s description and reference lists.
- Engelberg et al.'s chance-level claim vs. Lopez-Lira et al.'s high re-identification is unresolved in the sources read.

## 6. State of the art 2025–2026 and any consensus on what works best

### Takeaway
There is no single accepted fix. The 2025–2026 literature converges on four points: (1) entity/prior bias is real, scales with entity familiarity, and persists in frontier models; (2) prompt-only instructions and naive name masking are each insufficient on their own; (3) the most reliable way to *establish or rule out* the bias is counterfactual-pair testing plus an explicit re-identification check, with post-cutoff/unknown-entity data as the cleanest control; (4) white-box interventions (direction editing, attention/SAE steering) are the most promising mitigation frontier but are brittle across models and tasks and unavailable for closed APIs. Benchmarks comparing context-utilisation methods find that most techniques do not generalize across context types and look better on synthetic than on realistic data.

### Cited Findings
- **Hagström, Kim, Yu, Lee, Johansson, Cho, Augenstein (2025), "CUB: Benchmarking Context Utilisation Techniques for Language Models", ACL 2026, arXiv:2505.16518 [V-abs].** Seven context-utilisation manipulation techniques (representing the main categories) × three datasets × 11 LMs: "most existing CMTs struggle to handle the full spectrum of context types encountered in real-world RAG scenarios" and "many CMTs display inflated performance on simple synthesised datasets, compared to more realistic datasets". — [arXiv 2505.16518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16518)
- **Lou, Hu, Chen, Wang, Zhang, Pan (2026), "Which Knowledge Should LLMs Trust? Uncovering a Generalizable Steering Direction Across Knowledge Conflicts", arXiv:2610.20067 [V-abs; preprint].** The context-vs-parametric choice "is linearly represented and can be controlled along a single direction in the model's residual stream"; a direction learned on simple factual conflicts transfers to RAG, memory and rule-following tasks across four model families. — [arXiv 2610.20067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20067)
- Li, Chen, Tong (ICML 2025): memory and context signals are superposed in the same attention heads, undermining clean "memory head" ablation. — [arXiv 2503.10996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10996)
- Zhang et al. (2026): SAE steering works better with few causally selected features than with mass steering. — [arXiv 2609.08173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08173)
- **Bae et al. (2025), "That's Deprecated! Understanding, Detecting, and Steering Knowledge Conflicts in Language Models for Code Generation", arXiv:2510.19116 [V-abs]** — conflicts detectable with up to 80.65% accuracy by probes, but activation steering gains are modest ("up to a 12.6% improvement in steering success over a random baseline") and "effectiveness depends critically on balancing model size, task domain, and steering direction". — [arXiv 2510.19116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19116)
- **Lyu, Zhou, Toh, Zhu, Li (2026), "KC-Bench: A Dynamic Interactive Benchmark for Evaluating Knowledge Conflicts in LLM Agents", arXiv:2609.03588 [V-abs]** — across nine recent models, "no model handles factual correction, identity consistency checking, and temporal conflict resolution reliably across all settings". — [arXiv 2609.03588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03588)
- **Zhao, Chen, Zhang, Zhu, Lin, Liu (2026), "Exploring Knowledge Conflicts for Faithful LLM Reasoning: Benchmark and Method", SIGIR 2026, arXiv:2604.11209 [V-abs]** — with cross-source (text vs. KG) conflicts, LLMs "become more sensitive to prompting choices". — [arXiv 2604.11209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11209)
- Fairness side: Karvonen & Marks (2025) — prompts fail in realistic settings, affine concept editing robust (<2.5% residual bias); Ahsan & Wallace (ICLR 2026) — SAE steering "of marginal utility for realistic tasks"; Shan & Mueller (EACL 2026) — task-specific mechanisms, risk of "prior collapse"; Tripathy & Buckmann (2026) — output-level audits insufficient; AxBench (ICML 2025) — prompting beats SAE steering for concept steering. — [arXiv 2506.10922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10922), [arXiv 2511.00177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00177), [arXiv 2512.20796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20796), [arXiv 2605.15217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15217), [arXiv 2501.17148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17148)
- Finance side: masking and instructions both fail against memorization (Lopez-Lira et al. 2025); anonymization costs more signal than the bias it removes in some tasks (Wu et al. 2025); iterative LLM neutering with an adversarial identifier is the strongest masking variant reported (Engelberg et al. 2025 [S]; 9.04% residual firm identification in Wu et al.'s replication vs. 66.88% for NER masking); anonymization-first agent frameworks now ship with negative controls but without direct validation of the anonymization effect (Jeon & Lee 2026). — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14765), [arXiv 2511.15364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15364), [arXiv 2603.17692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17692)
- Training-time alternative noted for completeness (outside the "no retraining" scope): **Cohen, Carré, Lechtenbörger, Droste, Kerschke, Biswas, de Melo, Buys (2026), "Knowledgeless Language Models: Suppressing Parametric Recall for Evidence-Grounded Language Modeling", arXiv:2607.12831 [V-abs]** — pretraining on corpora with **anonymized named entities** reduces closed-book recall and gives "up to 20–25% relative gains" in retrieval-grounded settings with imperfect evidence, with better calibration and abstention. — [arXiv 2607.12831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12831)

### Inferences
- **Working consensus (my synthesis across the sources above, not a statement any single paper makes):** treat entity-prior bias as a measurement-validity problem first and a mitigation problem second. A credible pipeline layers (1) input-side neutralization (iterative mask-and-paraphrase or neighbor-entity placeholders, keeping decision-relevant numbers), (2) a reported manipulation check (re-identification rate by prominence stratum), (3) counterfactual invariance tests (name-absent, name-swapped, placebo-name and name-only arms with paired statistics and pre-registered thresholds), and (4) an out-of-memory control (post-cutoff filings or obscure/fictitious registrants). Instructions to "judge only from the text" are cheap to add but should not be counted as a control.
- Ranking by strength of evidence for *black-box* settings: counterfactual-pair testing (strong, many independent replications as a detection design) > iterative LLM neutering with adversarial identifier (promising; one primary paper [S] plus one independent replication, with a contested leak rate on long documents) > neighbor-entity in-context intervention (one paper, GPT-3.5, short tasks) ≈ opinion-style/counterfactual-demonstration prompting (one paper, older models) > plain "ignore" instructions (mixed to negative).
- For *white-box* settings, affine/direction editing has the best realistic-task evidence (Karvonen & Marks), while SAE-feature steering currently has more negative or qualified results than positive ones (AxBench; Ahsan & Wallace; Anthropic off-target effects; Zhang et al.'s critique of mass steering).
- The project's existing blinded round (masked names, matched industry pairs, pre-registered rules) aligns with the literature's recommended design elements — blind condition, matched pairs, pre-specification. The literature-backed additions it may lack are the re-identification manipulation check, the cross-swap and name-only arms, stratification by firm prominence, and an explicit estimate of information lost to masking.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 survey dedicated to *entity bias* (as opposed to knowledge conflicts or social bias) was found; the closest synthesis remains Xu et al. (EMNLP 2024).
- No head-to-head benchmark found comparing masking, neighbor substitution, prompting and steering on the same applied judgment task with frontier (2026) models.
- Several 2026 items are unreviewed preprints (Lou et al.; Zhang et al.; Lyu et al.; Tripathy & Buckmann; Cohen et al.; Yona et al.) and should be cited as such.
