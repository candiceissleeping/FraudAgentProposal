## 1. Definitions: what does "forgetting" formally mean? (exact vs. approximate; data points vs. knowledge/entities)

### Takeaway
"Unlearning" has a rigorous meaning only at the level of *training data*: the unlearned model should be (distributionally) indistinguishable from a model **retrained from scratch without the forget set** (exact unlearning; SISA) or approximately so (certified/approximate unlearning). LLM "unlearning" has drifted away from this: most LLM work targets *knowledge, entities, concepts or capabilities* whose forget set is not well-defined, lacks a retrained reference, and — per Cooper et al. (NeurIPS 2025), the 2025 SoK, and a 2026 ICML position paper — is better described as output **suppression/obfuscation** than removal.

### Cited Findings

**Foundational definitions (non-LLM)**
- **Cao & Yang (2015), "Towards Making Systems Forget with Machine Unlearning"**, IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2015, pp. 463–480, DOI 10.1109/SP.2015.35 (no arXiv). Coins "machine unlearning": convert learning algorithms to a *summation form* so that forgetting a sample only requires updating a small number of summations, "asymptotically faster than retraining from scratch"; goal is "forgetting systems" that forget data and its lineage "completely and quickly". Limitation: applies to statistical-query-style learners, not deep nets. (venue: confirmed) — [IEEE S&P 2015 PDF](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2015/papers-archived/6949a463.pdf); [IEEE CS proceedings index](https://computer.org/csdl/proceedings/sp/2015/6949/00/index.html)
- **Bourtoule, Chandrasekaran, Choquette-Choo, Jia, Travers, Zhang, Lie, Papernot (2021), "Machine Unlearning"**, IEEE S&P 2021, arXiv:1912.03817 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. Introduces **SISA training** (Sharded, Isolated, Sliced, Aggregated): limit each data point's influence to one shard/slice so that only the affected constituent model is retrained → *exact* unlearning by construction. Reported speed-ups over retraining from scratch: **4.63× (Purchase), 2.45× (SVHN), 1.36× (ImageNet, with transfer learning and "a small degradation in accuracy")**. Limitation: must be designed in at training time; speed-up shrinks for complex tasks; not practical for monolithic LLM pretraining. — [arXiv:1912.03817](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817)
- **Guo, Goldstein, Hannun, van der Maaten (2020), "Certified Data Removal from Machine Learning Models"**, ICML 2020, arXiv:1911.03030 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. Defines **certified removal**: "a very strong theoretical guarantee that a model from which data is removed cannot be distinguished from a model that never observed the data to begin with" (an (ε,δ)-indistinguishability notion); mechanism only for linear classifiers. — [arXiv:1911.03030](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03030)
- **Thudi, Jia, Shumailov, Papernot (2022), "On the Necessity of Auditable Algorithmic Definitions for Machine Unlearning"**, 31st USENIX Security Symposium, arXiv:2110.11891 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. States the standard dichotomy — *exact unlearning* = "formally removed the data point's impact on the model by retraining the model from scratch"; *approximate unlearning* = "approximates the model parameters one would obtain by exact unlearning". Shows the approximate definition is ill-posed ("one can obtain the same model using different datasets. Thus one could unlearn without modifying the model at all") and that one "cannot formally prove the absence of certain data points used during training"; concludes "unlearning is only well-defined at the algorithmic level". — [arXiv:2110.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11891)
- **Nguyen et al. (2022; rev. 2024), "A Survey of Machine Unlearning"**, arXiv:2209.02299 [V-arXiv] — general (pre-LLM) taxonomy reference. — [arXiv:2209.02299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)

**How LLM unlearning is framed**
- **Jang, Yoon, Yang, Cha, Lee, Logeswaran, Seo (2022/2023), "Knowledge Unlearning for Mitigating Privacy Risks in Language Models"**, arXiv:2210.01504 [V-arXiv]; ACL 2023 (venue: secondary listings only — an LG AI Research publication page and the WMDP reference list; ACL Anthology page not retrieved). Frames "knowledge unlearning" as post-hoc **gradient ascent on target token sequences**; claims it forgets them "with little to no degradation of general language modeling performances for larger LMs", that "sequential unlearning is better than trying to unlearn all the data at once", and that results are "highly dependent on which kind of data (domain) is forgotten". Scope: verbatim sequences vulnerable to extraction, known a priori — i.e., *instance-level*, not entity-level. — [arXiv:2210.01504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01504); [search result listing ACL 2023](https://www.lgresearch.ai/publication/view?seq=73)
- **Yao, Xu, Liu (2023/2024), "Large Language Model Unlearning"**, arXiv:2310.10683 [V-arXiv]; NeurIPS 2024 (venue: confirmed). Frames unlearning as an *alignment* technique for (1) removing harmful responses, (2) erasing copyrighted content, (3) reducing hallucinations; needs only negative examples; "unlearning can still achieve better alignment performance than RLHF with just 2% of its computational time". States priority is "to stop generating undesirable outputs" — i.e., an explicitly behavioural (suppression) goal. — [arXiv:2310.10683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10683); [NeurIPS 2024 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96383)
- **Si, Zhang, Chang, Zhang, Qu, Zhang (2023), "Knowledge Unlearning for LLMs: Tasks, Methods, and Challenges"**, arXiv:2311.15766 [V-arXiv; arXiv only]. Early survey; formally defines the knowledge-unlearning problem and groups methods into **parameter optimization, parameter merging, and in-context learning**. — [arXiv:2311.15766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15766)
- **Liu, Yao, Jia, Casper, Baracaldo, Hase, Yao, Liu, Xu, Li, Varshney, Bansal, Koyejo, Liu (2024/2025), "Rethinking Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models"**, arXiv:2402.08787 [V-arXiv]; Nature Machine Intelligence 7(2):181–194 (2025). Defines LLM unlearning as aiming "to eliminate undesirable data influence (e.g., sensitive or illegal information) **and the associated model capabilities**, while maintaining the integrity of essential knowledge generation and not affecting causally unrelated information"; highlights "often-overlooked aspects": **unlearning scope, data-model interaction, and multifaceted efficacy assessment**; connects unlearning to model editing, influence functions, adversarial training. Motivation is "without the need of full retraining". — [arXiv:2402.08787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08787); [NSF PAR record for the Nat. Mach. Intell. version](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10596967)
- **Maini et al. (2024), TOFU** (see §3) gives the operational LLM definition used by benchmarks: a model trained on S and unlearned on S_F "should behave like a model trained only on S_R"; target is models that "truly behave as if they were never trained on the forget data at all". — [arXiv:2401.06121](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06121v1)
- **Shi et al. (2024), MUSE** (see §3): "exactly unlearning only these datapoints (i.e., retraining with the data removed) is intractable in modern-day models. This has led to the development of many approximate unlearning algorithms." — [arXiv:2407.06460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06460)

**Conceptual critiques of the definition**
- **Cooper et al. (2024/2025), "Machine Unlearning Doesn't Do What You Think: Lessons for Generative AI Policy and Research"**, arXiv:2412.06966 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment "NeurIPS 2025 (Oral)"]. Separates two goals: **(a) targeted removal of information from a model's parameters** and **(b) targeted suppression of information in a model's outputs**. Removal "applies to discrete pieces of data in the training dataset before training occurs"; "it cannot target latent patterns a trained model has learned across different data". Retraining from scratch (the "gold standard") "only directly targets information that is directly observable in the training data". Named mismatches: "Output suppression is not a replacement for removal of training data"; "Removal of training data does not guarantee meaningful output suppression"; "Removing information from model parameters does not give guarantees about model outputs" because models "generalize beyond the information that is exactly contained in their training data"; a model is not equivalent to its outputs, nor to downstream uses. Bottom line: unlearning "is not a general-purpose solution for circumscribing generative-AI model behavior"; "we are doubtful that unlearning methods will deliver full compliance in the future." — [arXiv:2412.06966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06966); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06966v2)
- **Thaker, Hu, Kale, Maurya, Wu, Smith (2024/2025), "Position: LLM Unlearning Benchmarks are Weak Measures of Progress"**, arXiv:2410.02879 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment, IEEE SaTML 2025]. "existing benchmarks provide an overly optimistic and potentially misleading view"; with "simple, benign modifications" to popular benchmarks, "supposedly unlearned information remains accessible", or retained-information utility is degraded far more than reported; benchmarks are "particularly vulnerable to modifications that introduce even loose dependencies between the forget and retain information"; "ambiguity in unlearning targets" lets methods "overfit to the given test queries". — [arXiv:2410.02879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02879)
- **Yoon, Jun, No (2026), "Position: The Term "Machine Unlearning" Is Overused in LLMs"**, arXiv:2606.27379 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment, ICML 2026 Position Paper Track]. Argues the term "should be reserved for dataset-defined deletion: removing the training influence of a precisely specified forget set such that the resulting model is approximately indistinguishable from retraining without that data"; tasks like "refusal for harmful requests, **entity/knowledge removal**, or targeted suppression" pursue different objectives and need different terminology ("alignment, suppression, editing, obfuscation"); current metrics reward "surface-level non-disclosure (e.g., low ROUGE/forget accuracy) even when retraining-equivalence is not tested and derived capabilities remain." — [arXiv:2606.27379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27379)

**Data-point vs. entity/concept/knowledge unlearning**
- **Ma, Feng, Zhong, Huang, Ye, Feng, Qin (2024/2025), "Unveiling Entity-Level Unlearning for Large Language Models: A Comprehensive Analysis"**, arXiv:2406.15796 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment, COLING 2025]. Distinguishes *instance-level* unlearning ("removal of predefined instances") from *entity-level* unlearning ("erase entity-related knowledge from the target model completely"). Finds "current methods struggle to achieve effective entity-level unlearning"; "knowledge coverage and the size of the forget set play pivotal roles"; and "**entities introduced through fine-tuning are more vulnerable to unlearning than pre-trained entities**". — [arXiv:2406.15796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15796)
- **Liu, Zhang, Jaakkola, Chang (2024), "Revisiting Who's Harry Potter: Towards Targeted Unlearning from a Causal Intervention Perspective"**, arXiv:2407.16997 [V-arXiv; venue not verified]. Defines *targeted unlearning*: given an unlearning target (e.g., a person) and documents, "unlearn only the information about the target, rather than everything in the unlearning documents"; success criteria include "not outputting gibberish, not fabricating facts about the unlearning target, and not releasing factual information under jailbreak attacks". — [arXiv:2407.16997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16997)
- **Jin et al. (2024), RWKU** (see §3): entity-level setting on 200 real-world famous people where "neither the forget corpus nor the retain corpus is accessible". — [arXiv:2406.10890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10890)
- **Tian et al. (2024), KnowUnDo** (see §3): "current unlearning paradigms are mired in vague forgetting boundaries, often erasing knowledge indiscriminately." — [arXiv:2407.01920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01920)
- **Ren et al. (2025), "SoK: Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models"** (see §5): proposes an *intention-based* taxonomy — whether a method "seeks to truly remove internal knowledge or merely suppress its behavioral effects". — [arXiv:2506.09227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09227)

### Inferences
- "Make the model not know what later happened to Enron/WorldCom" is **entity-/event-level knowledge unlearning of pretrained knowledge with no enumerable forget set** — the furthest point from the well-defined data-deletion setting. Under the Thudi/Yoon definitions it is not "unlearning" at all but suppression/editing; under Cooper et al. it targets *latent* information spread across thousands of documents (news, textbooks, case law, later 10-Ks of other firms that mention "Enron"), which removal-style methods "cannot target".
- The only formally meaningful guarantee for "the model never saw post-T information" is the exact-unlearning one — i.e., a model whose training corpus excluded post-T text (retrain-from-scratch / point-in-time training). All approximate LLM methods can at best be *audited empirically*, and the definition they approximate is itself contested (Thudi et al.).

### Gaps
- No paper found that gives a formal definition of *temporal* unlearning ("forget everything after date T") with an indistinguishability-to-retrained-model criterion. The closest are point-in-time training papers (§6), which achieve it by construction rather than by unlearning.
- ACL 2023 venue for Jang et al. was confirmed only through secondary listings; ACL Anthology page was not retrieved.

## 2. Method families and representative papers

### Takeaway
LLM unlearning methods fall into (i) loss-reversal fine-tuning (GA / GradDiff / KL / NPO / SimNPO), (ii) relabel-and-finetune (WHP, IDK/rejection tuning), (iii) representation misdirection (RMU) and steering/SAE interventions, (iv) weight arithmetic and editing (task vectors, ROME/MEMIT, LEACE, pruning/localization), (v) inference-time/guardrail methods that never touch the weights (in-context unlearning, prompts/filters, ECO, logit-offset methods such as δ-Unlearning/ULD/Divergence Decoding), and (vi) robustness-oriented training (LAT, TAR, SAM, gradient routing, distillation/UNDO). The 2025 SoK classifies most of (ii)–(v) as *suppression-intended* and argues even the *removal-intended* family (i) behaves like suppression. Only two works were found that target a **knowledge cutoff** — one prompt-based (fails on causally related knowledge) and one finance-specific logit-adjustment method — plus one decoding-time finance method (FinCAD).

### Cited Findings

**(i) Gradient ascent / gradient difference / KL / NPO family**
- **GA** — Jang et al. 2022 (above): maximize loss on forget sequences. — [arXiv:2210.01504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01504)
- **Gradient Difference, KL Minimization, Preference Optimization ("I don't know" tuning)** — defined as TOFU baselines: GradDiff "maximizes forget-set loss while minimizing retain-set loss"; KL keeps retain-set output distribution close to the original model; PO trains forget questions toward "I don't know"-style answers. — [arXiv:2401.06121](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06121v1)
- **Zhang, Lin, Bai, Mei (2024), "Negative Preference Optimization: From Catastrophic Collapse to Effective Unlearning"**, arXiv:2404.05868 [V-arXiv; venue: COLM 2024 from memory, NOT verified this session]. GA methods "either fail to effectively unlearn the target data or suffer from catastrophic collapse"; NPO is a bounded, alignment-inspired loss; "the progression toward catastrophic collapse by minimizing the NPO loss is exponentially slower than GA"; on TOFU "NPO-based methods are the first to achieve reasonable unlearning results in forgetting 50% (or more) of the training data, whereas existing methods already struggle with forgetting 10%". Limitation: evaluated on TOFU (fine-tuned fictitious knowledge); later shown reversible (§4). — [arXiv:2404.05868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05868)
- **Fan, Liu, Lin, Jia, Zhang, Mei, Liu (2024/2025), "Simplicity Prevails: Rethinking Negative Preference Optimization for LLM Unlearning" (SimNPO)**, arXiv:2410.07163 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment, NeurIPS 2025]. Identifies NPO's "reference model bias"; SimNPO removes reliance on the reference model; validated on TOFU and MUSE and for "robustness against relearning attacks". — [arXiv:2410.07163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07163)
- **Yao, Chien, Du, Niu, Wang, Cheng, Yue (2024), "Machine Unlearning of Pre-trained Large Language Models"**, arXiv:2402.15159 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment, ACL 2024 main]. Benchmarks seven methods on arXiv/books/GitHub data from *pretraining*; methods are "over 10^5 times more computationally efficient than retraining"; "integrating gradient ascent with gradient descent on in-distribution data improves hyperparameter robustness". — [arXiv:2402.15159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15159)
- **Jia et al. (2024), "SOUL: Unlocking the Power of Second-Order Optimization for LLM Unlearning"**, arXiv:2404.18239 [V-arXiv; venue not verified] — second-order optimizer variant. — [arXiv:2404.18239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18239)
- **Yuan, Pang, Du, Chen, Zhang, Lin (2024/2025), "A Closer Look at Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models"**, arXiv:2410.08109 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment, ICLR 2025]. Splits methods into *untargeted* vs *targeted*; "the behavior that untargeted unlearning attempts to approximate is unpredictable and may involve hallucinations"; proposes maximizing entropy (ME) and answer-preservation (AP) loss. — [arXiv:2410.08109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08109)
- **Chen & Yang (2023), "Unlearn What You Want to Forget: Efficient Unlearning for LLMs"**, EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2310.20150 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment] — lightweight unlearning layers/adapters. **Wang et al. (2023), "KGA: A General Machine Unlearning Framework Based on Knowledge Gap Alignment"**, ACL 2023, arXiv:2305.06535 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. — [arXiv:2310.20150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20150); [arXiv:2305.06535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06535)

**(ii) Relabel-and-finetune: "Who's Harry Potter?"**
- **Eldan & Russinovich (2023), "Who's Harry Potter? Approximate Unlearning in LLMs"**, arXiv:2310.02238 [V-arXiv; arXiv only as far as verified]. Three components: (1) a *reinforced model* further trained on target data to identify target-related tokens by logit comparison with a baseline; (2) replace idiosyncratic expressions with generic counterparts and use the model's own predictions as *alternative labels* approximating "the next-token predictions of a model that has not been trained on the target data"; (3) fine-tune on those labels. Llama2-7b: "took over 184K GPU-hours to pretrain"; "in about 1 GPU hour of finetuning, we effectively erase the model's ability to generate or recall Harry Potter-related content" with benchmark performance "almost unaffected". Limitations: see Lynch et al. (§3/§4) — knowledge remains extractable. — [arXiv:2310.02238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02238)

**(iii) Representation misdirection: RMU**
- **Li, Pan, Gopal, Yue, Berrios, Gatti, … Hendrycks (2024), "The WMDP Benchmark: Measuring and Reducing Malicious Use With Unlearning"**, arXiv:2403.03218 [V-arXiv]; ICML 2024, PMLR 235:28525–28550 (venue: confirmed). **RMU** (Representation Misdirection for Unlearning): forget loss steers activations on hazardous data at layer ℓ toward a scaled fixed random vector; retain loss is an ℓ2 penalty keeping activations on benign data (WikiText) near the frozen model; L = L_forget + α·L_retain; updates only layers ℓ−2..ℓ. Results (before→after RMU): zephyr-7b WMDP-Bio 63.7→31.2, WMDP-Cyber 44.0→28.2, MMLU 58.1→57.1; Yi-34b Bio 75.3→30.7, MMLU 72.6→70.6; Mixtral-8x7B Bio 74.8→34.0, MMLU 68.2→67.1 (25% = chance). Robustness claims: linear probes "only … slightly better than random"; GCG on unlearned model outputs "gibberish even after 2,500 steps". Stated limits: "finetuning after unlearning is not covered by our threat model"; RMU "perhaps obfuscates knowledge more than unlearns it". — [arXiv:2403.03218](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03218v7); [ICML 2024 entry](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2024/li2024icml-wmdp/)

**(iv) Weight arithmetic, model editing, concept erasure, localization/pruning**
- **Ilharco, Ribeiro, Wortsman, Gururangan, Schmidt, Hajishirzi, Farhadi (2022/2023), "Editing Models with Task Arithmetic"**, ICLR 2023, arXiv:2212.04089 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. Task vector = fine-tuned weights − pretrained weights; "Negating a task vector decreases performance on the target task, with little change in model behavior on control tasks". As an unlearning method in MUSE it strongly *under*-unlearns (PrivLeak −99.8 on News; §3). — [arXiv:2212.04089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)
- **Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov (2022), "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT" (ROME)**, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2202.05262 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. Causal tracing locates factual recall in "middle-layer feed-forward modules … while processing subject tokens"; Rank-One Model Editing rewrites one association. — [arXiv:2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)
- **Meng, Sen Sharma, Andonian, Belinkov, Bau (2022/2023), "Mass-Editing Memory in a Transformer" (MEMIT)**, arXiv:2210.07229 [V-arXiv; venue: ICLR 2023 from memory, NOT verified this session]. Scales direct editing "up to thousands of associations for GPT-J (6B) and GPT-NeoX (20B)". — [arXiv:2210.07229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229)
- **Belrose, Schneider-Joseph, Ravfogel, Cotterell, Raff, Biderman (2023), "LEACE: Perfect linear concept erasure in closed form"**, arXiv:2306.03819 [V-arXiv; venue: NeurIPS 2023 from memory, NOT verified this session]. Closed-form method that "provably prevents all linear classifiers from detecting a concept while changing the embedding as little as possible"; "concept scrubbing" applies it at every layer. Demonstrated on part-of-speech and gender in BERT — a *linear, attribute-level* guarantee, not removal of factual knowledge about an entity. — [arXiv:2306.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819)
- **Wu et al. (2023), "DEPN: Detecting and Editing Privacy Neurons in Pretrained Language Models"**, EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2310.20138 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment] — locate "privacy neurons" and zero their activations. — [arXiv:2310.20138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20138)
- **Pochinkov & Schoots (2024), "Dissecting Language Models: Machine Unlearning via Selective Pruning"**, arXiv:2403.01267 [V-arXiv; venue not verified] — prune neurons by relative importance to a targeted capability vs. overall performance. — [arXiv:2403.01267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01267)
- **Guo, Syed, Sheshadri, Ewart, Dziugaite (2024), "Mechanistic Unlearning: Robust Knowledge Unlearning and Editing via Mechanistic Localization"**, arXiv:2410.12949 [V-arXiv; venue not verified]. Localizing edits to the "lookup-table mechanism for factual recall" gives "more robust edits/unlearning across different input/output formats" and "resists attempts to relearn the unwanted information" (sports-facts and CounterFact datasets); output-preserving localization (e.g., causal tracing) is less robust. — [arXiv:2410.12949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12949)
- **Tian et al. (2024), MemFlex** (KnowUnDo paper): "utilizes gradient information to precisely target and unlearn sensitive parameters". — [arXiv:2407.01920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01920)
- **Cloud, Goldman-Wetzler, Wybitul, Miller, Turner (2024), "Gradient Routing: Masking Gradients to Localize Computation in Neural Networks"**, arXiv:2410.04332 [V-arXiv; venue not verified]. A *training-time* method: data-dependent gradient masks isolate capabilities into a network subregion, enabling "robust unlearning via ablation of a pre-specified network subregion". Requires routing during (pre)training — not a post-hoc method. — [arXiv:2410.04332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04332)

**(v) Inference-time / guardrail / prompt-based "unlearning" (weights unchanged)**

- **Zhao, Brumbaugh, Wang, Hajishirzi, Smith (2024), "Set the Clock: Temporal Alignment of Pretrained Language Models"**, Findings of ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.16797 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. ### Inferences
- Every method in families (ii), (iii), (v) and SAE guardrails leaves the underlying knowledge in the weights by design; the SoK (§5) classifies them as suppression-intended. For a fraud-scoring pipeline with a *non-adversarial* user this may sound acceptable, but the failure mode that matters here is not jailbreaks — it is **implicit use** of the knowledge when it is "not directly asked but causally related" (Gao et al.), which is exactly the 10-K-scoring situation (the prompt never asks "did Enron collapse?").
- Classifier-gated methods (ECO, DSG, ARIA) need a forget-scope detector. For "everything about entity X after date T" the scope is fuzzy (later filings by counterparties, auditor Arthur Andersen, SOX commentary), so recall of the gate is the binding constraint.
- Logit-offset methods (δ-Unlearning, ULD, Divergence Decoding) are the only family with a finance/knowledge-cutoff instantiation; evidence that they neutralize *outcome* knowledge in a downstream judgement task (rather than verbatim/QA recall) was not found.

- No paper was found that performs **weight-level temporal unlearning** ("remove all knowledge after date T") on an LLM and validates it against a time-truncated retrained model. The time-unlearn evidence is prompt-level only (Gao et al.) or inference-time logit adjustment (Merchant & Levy; FinCAD).

## 3. Benchmarks and metrics: how is success measured?

### Takeaway
The standard yardstick is a two-axis trade-off — **forget quality** (ideally: statistical indistinguishability from a retain-only/retrained model) vs. **model utility** — as instantiated by TOFU, MUSE, WMDP, RWKU and KnowUnDo, now unified in OpenUnlearning. All of these are behavioural; a strong line of meta-evaluation work (Lynch et al., Thaker et al., Hong et al., Feng et al., Pal et al., BLUR) shows benchmark scores are weak evidence of removal, and that TOFU/MUSE-style setups unlearn *fine-tuned* knowledge, which is easier to remove than pretrained knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- **Maini, Feng, Schwarzschild, Lipton, Kolter (2024), "TOFU: A Task of Fictitious Unlearning for LLMs"**, arXiv:2401.06121 [V-arXiv; venue: COLM 2024 from memory, NOT verified this session; project site unreachable]. 200 fictitious author profiles × 20 QA pairs (4,000 QA, GPT-4 generated); forget splits 1%/5%/10% (2/10/20 authors); models (Llama-2-7B, Phi-1.5) are first *fine-tuned* on TOFU. **Forget Quality** = p-value of a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test comparing the *Truth Ratio* distribution on the forget set between the unlearned model and a **retain-only model** (high p = indistinguishable). **Model Utility** = harmonic mean of nine numbers (probability, ROUGE-L recall, rescaled truth ratio × Retain Set, Real Authors, World Facts). Findings: "none of the baselines we consider show effective unlearning"; even at 1% forget, p-values stayed below 0.05; all methods reduced utility and often produced gibberish within two epochs; "existing methods are weak attempts at unlearning". Limitation: knowledge is synthetic and injected by fine-tuning. — [arXiv:2401.06121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06121); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06121v1)
- **Shi, Lee, Huang, Malladi, Zhao, Holtzman, Liu, Zettlemoyer, Smith, Zhang (2024), "MUSE: Machine Unlearning Six-Way Evaluation for Language Models"**, arXiv:2407.06460 [V-arXiv; venue: ICLR 2025 from memory, NOT verified this session; arXiv v2 says "Under review"]. ### Inferences
- A benchmark only supports the claim "equivalent to never having seen the data" if it includes a retrained/retain-only reference (TOFU's KS test; MUSE's f_retrain; PrivLeak). No such reference exists for "an LLM that never learned what happened to Enron", unless one trains a time-truncated model — which is the alternative under comparison (§6).
- TOFU and MUSE measure removal of knowledge *added by fine-tuning*; Ma et al. and Deeb & Roger (§1, §4) both find pretrained knowledge harder to remove. Enron/WorldCom knowledge is pretrained, high-frequency, multi-hop-connected knowledge — the regime where benchmark success transfers least.
- For the fraud project, the relevant test is "downstream task awareness" (Feng et al.): does the *fraud score* for the FY2000 10-K change relative to a no-knowledge reference? None of the standard metrics (ROUGE, MCQ accuracy, MIA AUC) measure this.

## 4. Robustness critiques: does unlearning truly remove knowledge?

### Takeaway
The evidence from 2023–2026 is consistent: for knowledge acquired in pretraining, current approximate unlearning **hides rather than removes**. "Forgotten" knowledge has been recovered by (a) fine-tuning on a handful of related *or unrelated* examples, (b) retraining on a disjoint subset of the forgotten facts (≥88% recovery), (c) 4-bit quantization (21%→83% retained), (d) activation-direction ablation, logit lens, and adversarial prompts, and (e) simply re-supplying information in context. One methodological caveat (Feng et al. 2025): some recovery evaluations inject information and may overstate how shallow unlearning is.

### Cited Findings

**Relearning / fine-tuning attacks**
- **Hu, Fu, Wu, Smith (2024/2025), "Unlearning or Obfuscating? Jogging the Memory of Unlearned LLMs via Benign Relearning"**, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2406.13356 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. Threat model: adversary has the unlearned model and can fine-tune on a small auxiliary set D′ that is either a limited subset of forget data or *public, loosely related* information that does not answer the evaluation queries. Benchmarks: TOFU (Phi-1.5), WHP (Llama-2-7b), WMDP (zephyr-7b-beta, Llama-3-8b); methods: GA, GD, KL, NPO, SCRUB, RMU. Results: attack success rate >70% on TOFU and WHP under shallow unlearning; WMDP LLM-judge score (1–10): original 5.92; GA 1.67→5.2, GD 1.09→5.2, NPO 1→5.08 after relearning (relearn set built from 15 questions). "relearning on public medical articles can lead an unlearned LLM to output harmful knowledge about bioweapons, and relearning general wiki information about the book series Harry Potter can force the model to output verbatim memorized text." Conclusion: "current approximate unlearning methods simply suppress the model outputs and fail to robustly forget target knowledge". Limitation: deeper unlearning lowers ASR (at utility cost). — [arXiv:2406.13356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13356); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13356v4)

**Counter-evidence / caveats**
- Feng et al. 2025 (§3): some "unlearning is shallow" findings come from evaluations that "introduce substantial new information into the model … re-teaching the model during testing". Deeb & Roger's RTT is designed to address this by testing on held-out facts V "that cannot be guessed from the accessible facts", with a control (a model never fine-tuned on Random Birthdays peaks at 31.2% under RTT). — [arXiv:2506.00688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00688); [arXiv:2410.08827 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08827v3)
- Robustness is better for knowledge that was *fine-tuned in* (Deeb & Roger: GD/RIA recovery <35% on Random Birthdays) and for mechanistically localized edits (Guo et al. 2024) and direct concept-vector ablation (Hong et al.). — [arXiv:2410.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08827); [arXiv:2410.12949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12949); [arXiv:2406.11614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11614)

### Inferences
- The fraud-scoring use case does not involve an attacker fine-tuning the model, so relearning attacks are not the operative threat. They matter as **diagnostics**: if 10 unrelated examples or 4-bit rounding restore the knowledge, the knowledge is still encoded and can plausibly still shape a holistic judgement (fraud risk score) through pathways the unlearning objective never penalized. Hong et al. and Xu et al. give representation-level support for "suppressed, not erased".
- The **ununlearning** argument applies with unusual force here: the input document *is* the Enron FY2000 10-K, full of entity-identifying context (name, SPE structures, auditor). Even a model with degraded direct recall is being handed a strong retrieval cue; Hu et al. show loosely related text suffices to "jog" memory under fine-tuning, and Gao et al. show causally related queries defeat prompt-level cutoffs. Whether *in-context* cues alone (no fine-tuning) reactivate weight-unlearned entity knowledge is not directly measured in the papers reviewed.
- **Ripple effects** imply that unlearning "Enron went bankrupt in December 2001" leaves a web of entailed facts (Arthur Andersen's collapse, Sarbanes-Oxley's origin, "Enron-style accounting" as an idiom, Skilling/Lay convictions, mark-to-market/SPE red-flag lore that was *derived from* Enron). Complete, consistent removal would also strip general fraud-detection knowledge that post-dates and derives from these cases — an entanglement problem with the model's task competence itself.

### Gaps
- No study found measuring whether weight-unlearned knowledge still influences **downstream judgement tasks** (classification/scoring) when not directly queried — the specific mechanism of concern for look-ahead bias. Gao et al. test this only for prompt-based cutoffs.
- The earlier-version titles of Hu et al. and Zhang et al. were not re-verified against arXiv v1 pages.

## 5. State of the art in 2025–2026: has any method achieved robust knowledge removal?

### Takeaway
No post-hoc method has been shown to robustly remove pretrained knowledge from an LLM. The 2025 SoK concludes "current methods suppress rather than erase internal representations"; 2026 papers still report every tested method failing 20-example relearning. The most credible robustness result — **UNDO ("Distillation Robustifies Unlearning", NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight)** — works precisely *because* it re-trains a (partially) fresh network, costs 60–80% of full retraining at its strongest setting, and was demonstrated mainly on synthetic tasks; its key negative result is that even an output-perfect imitation of a retrained oracle relearns fast. Robustness-oriented training (LAT, TAR, SAM, layered unlearning, minor-component unlearning, margin calibration) improves but does not close the gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Lee, Foote, Infanger, Shor, Kamath, Goldman-Wetzler, Woodworth, Cloud, Turner (2025), "Distillation Robustifies Unlearning"**, NeurIPS 2025 (Spotlight), arXiv:2506.06278 [V-arXiv; venue: arXiv comment]. (1) Negative result: "Current LLM unlearning methods are not robust. A few steps of finetuning can revert their effects. … this is true even for an idealized form of unlearning: training to imitate a model that was never trained on unwanted information. This shows that training a model can drastically modify its input-output behavior while leaving its underlying capabilities intact." (2) Positive result: "Training a randomly initialized student on the outputs of an unlearned model transfers behaviors while leaving latent capabilities behind." (3) **UNDO** (Unlearn-Noise-Distill-on-Outputs): unlearn → perturb weights toward noise (mixing coefficient α; α=1 ≈ random re-initialization) → distill the damaged student back to the teacher's outputs; "a tunable tradeoff between compute cost and robustness". "At its strongest setting, UNDO matches the robustness of a model retrained from scratch with perfect data filtering while using only **60–80% of the compute** and requiring only **0.01% of the pretraining data to be labeled**." Relearning evaluation: 500 fine-tuning steps, learning-rate sweep, worst-case adversary. WMDP with Gemma-2-2B: α=0.25, distilled on only "0.015% of the size of the original pretraining corpus" (authors say they "undertrain"); "the UNDO model achieves **4.88% lower on MMLU** on average". Limitations (authors'): "introduces substantial computational costs, as we are training a student model from scratch"; "considerably more costly than finetuning-based unlearning methods"; main Pareto results are on **synthetic language and arithmetic tasks**. — [arXiv:2506.06278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06278); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06278v3)
- **Ren, Xing, Cui, Aggarwal, et al. (2025), "SoK: Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models"**, arXiv:2506.09227 [V-arXiv; venue not verified]. Intention-based taxonomy: *removal-intended* (GA family incl. ### Inferences
- The frontier has converged on a structural conclusion: robustness to recovery tracks **how much of the network was re-trained without the unwanted information**. UNDO interpolates between cheap/suppressive (α→0) and retrain-from-scratch (α→1); gradient routing needs training-time intervention. The "robust" end of the spectrum is, in effect, retraining.
- UNDO's oracle-matching result is the single most important finding for the fraud project: *matching the outputs of a model that never saw the information does not make a model equivalent to it*. Hence passing a behavioural "does it still know about Enron?" quiz cannot certify that latent knowledge is gone.
- No 2025–2026 result demonstrates robust removal of a **famous real-world entity's history** from a frontier-scale LLM with utility preserved; reported robust wins are on synthetic tasks, TOFU-style fine-tuned facts, sports-fact lookups, or WMDP with measurable utility loss.

### Gaps
- No independent third-party replication of UNDO or of 2026 robust-unlearning preprints (MCU, Margin Calibration, ARIA) was reviewed; several are un-refereed arXiv preprints.
- Full text of the 2025–2026 surveys (Geng et al., Qiu et al.) was not read beyond abstracts; their detailed conclusions on robustness are not quoted here.

## 6. Practical implication: unlearning vs. training from scratch on time-truncated data for "making a model not know an entity's future"

### Takeaway
For the goal "the model must not know what happened to Enron/WorldCom after the filing date", the literature supports **time-truncated (point-in-time) training as the only approach with a by-construction guarantee**, and offers no evidence that unlearning can deliver an equivalent. Unlearning is (a) ill-defined for this target, (b) empirically reversible/suppressive for pretrained knowledge, (c) untested at the weight level for temporal cutoffs, and (d) entangled with the fraud-detection competence itself. The cost is capability: available point-in-time models are small (≈0.15–4B parameters), and only ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT have vintages as early as 1999–2001.

**What the unlearning literature implies for this comparison**
- Exact unlearning *is* retraining without the data (Thudi et al.; Bourtoule et al.); approximate unlearning's own definition is contested. — [arXiv:2110.11891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11891)
- Retraining "only directly targets information that is directly observable in the training data", but this is precisely what a date cutoff exploits: all post-T text is excluded, so no post-T *observed* information exists to generalize from. — [arXiv:2412.06966](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06966v2)
- Entity-level unlearning of pretrained entities is the weak case (Ma et al.); pretrained facts — including dated events — are ≥88% recoverable after GD/RMU/RIA (Deeb & Roger). — [arXiv:2406.15796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15796); [arXiv:2410.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08827)
- Even output-matching a retrained oracle leaves latent capability; robust removal required distilling into a (largely) re-initialized network at 60–80% of retraining compute (Lee et al.). — [arXiv:2506.06278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06278)
- Prompted cutoffs fail on causally related queries (≈19% success) (Gao et al.). — [arXiv:2510.02340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02340)
- Inference-time logit adjustment is the only unlearning-style tool with a finance instantiation; its authors concede that training from scratch with a cutoff is "the simplest solution" and motivate their method by cost only (Merchant & Levy). — [arXiv:2512.06607](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06607v2)
- A search-result summary of Li et al. 2026 (FinCAD) attributes to that paper the observation that weight-level unlearning for backtests would need "a bespoke unlearning pass per ticker per evaluation date" (snippet-level; not verified in full text). — [arXiv:2605.24564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564)
- Unlearning degrades with scale and sequence: MUSE finds algorithms "cannot sustainably accommodate successive unlearning requests or large-scale content removal"; the SoK reports per-step utility loss "often much greater than 3%". — [arXiv:2407.06460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06460); [arXiv:2506.09227](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09227v1)

### Inferences
Side-by-side for the fraud-scoring project (Enron FY2000, WorldCom FY2001, …):

| Criterion | Post-hoc unlearning of a frontier LLM | Time-truncated (point-in-time) model |
|---|---|---|
| Guarantee that post-T outcome knowledge is absent | None; only empirical audits, which are known to be over-optimistic (§3–4) | By construction, subject to timestamp hygiene of the corpus (Cheng et al.) |
| Definition of forget set | Open-ended ("everything about entity X after T", plus ripple facts); cannot be enumerated | Trivial: drop all text dated after T |
| Evidence base | No weight-level temporal-unlearning study; prompt-level fails on causal queries; pretrained facts ≥88% recoverable | Multiple model families with probing/perplexity checks (ChronoGPT, DatedGPT, Kelly et al.) |
| Residual implicit influence on scoring | Likely (suppression ≠ removal; UNDO oracle result) | None from post-T text |
| Collateral damage | Entangled: fraud red-flag knowledge partly *derives from* Enron/WorldCom; over-unlearning risks removing task competence, under-unlearning leaves bias | Model also lacks post-T *general* knowledge (e.g., SOX-era fraud heuristics) — which is the correct counterfactual for "judging fairly as of T" |
| Capability | Frontier-level reasoning retained (minus utility loss) | Small models: 149M–4B parameters; ChronoGPT context 1,792 tokens — a 10-K must be chunked |
| Coverage of 2000–2001 cutoffs | n/a | ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT vintages 1999–2024 cover it; DatedGPT and Kelly et al. start at 2013 and do **not** |
| Cost for many firms/dates | One unlearning run (and audit) per entity × date, with sequential-unlearning degradation | One model per vintage serves all firms at that date |

- The defensible framing for the literature review: unlearning is, at present, a **suppression technology whose success cannot be certified**, so it cannot underwrite a claim that an LLM "judges the FY2000 10-K without hindsight". Point-in-time models can, at a capability cost. A hybrid design suggested by the evidence is to use a point-in-time model as the *reference/control* (lower bound free of leakage, per He et al.) and to treat any unlearned, anonymized, or prompt-restricted frontier model as an *upper-bound/contaminated* arm whose gap to the control quantifies hindsight bias (analogous to DatedGPT's "lookahead premium" and Wu et al.'s "anonymization gap").
- UNDO-style distillation could in principle produce a robustly "amnesic" model, but it requires an unlearned teacher whose *outputs* already exclude all post-T knowledge on the distillation corpus, and near-pretraining-scale compute — at which point direct time-truncated training is simpler and carries a cleaner guarantee.
- Inference-time methods (Divergence Decoding, FinCAD, ECO-style gating) are the practical way to *attenuate* hindsight in a frontier model, but they are suppression methods and inherit the "causally related knowledge" failure documented by Gao et al.; they should be validated on the downstream score, not on recall quizzes.

### Gaps
- **No head-to-head study** was found comparing an unlearned frontier LLM against a time-truncated model on the same downstream historical-judgement task. This comparison is a genuine open question the project could contribute to.
- No point-in-time model at frontier scale exists in the reviewed literature; the largest is 4B parameters (Kelly et al. 2026), and its vintages start in 2013.
- "Entity Neutering" (Engelberg et al. 2025) could not be verified against a primary source (SSRN not reached; `negocios.uchile.cl` unreachable from this session).
- Whether ChronoGPT's 1999–2001 vintages have enough language competence to read 10-K-length financial disclosures for fraud cues is untested in the sources reviewed (HellaSwag ≈37%; 1,792-token context).
- TiMaGPT's cutoff years and StoriesLM details were not verified this session.
