[REMOVED since Year T-1] The effects of the new Medicare legislation on an entity’s financial position and results of operations are normally required to be reflected in the period in which the legislation is enacted. However, the FASB has permitted entities to defer the accounting for the effects of the legislation. Accordingly, we have the option either to account for the effects of the legislation in our Year T-1 financial statements or to defer our accounting until Year T. Because implementing regulations under the legislation have not been issued and the implications of the legislation’s provisions on our retiree healthcare programs in the U.S. need to be fully assessed, we have elected to defer the accounting for the changes in Medicare. We will account for the effects of this legislation in the period in which authoritative guidance on the accounting for the federal subsidy is issued unless an earlier significant event occurs, such as a plan amendment or curtailment, that would
call for a remeasurement of our plans’ obligations. If such a significant event were to occur before the authoritative guidance were issued, we would reflect our best estimate of the effects of the legislation, including the federal subsidy, at that time. While we expect the effect of this legislation to positively affect our financial condition and results of operations, we will be unable to quantify the full impact until the completion of the assessment.
[ADDED in Year T] In May Year T-1, the FASB Emerging Issues Task Force (EITF) issued EITF [#], “Accounting for Cash Balance Pension Plans.” EITF [#] addresses whether a cash balance retirement plan should be considered a defined contribution plan or a defined benefit plan for purposes of applying SFAS No. [#], “Employers’ Accounting for Pensions,” and, if considered a defined benefit plan, the appropriate expense attribution method. The EITF included a consensus that cash balance plans are defined benefit plans for purposes of applying SFAS No. [#] and that such plans should apply the unit credit method for determining expense associated with the plans. A substantial majority of our domestic pension plans are cash balance pension plans that have been considered to be defined benefit plans for purposes of applying SFAS No. [#]. method. Beginning in Year T, our pension expense for cash balance plans has been determined using the unit credit method.
