Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations - ---------------------------------------------------------- Results of Operations, General: The Company since Year T-6 has derived a significantly greater percentage of its revenue from government R & D contracts. Once a contract is signed, the government can not reduce the contract price. The government may, however upon notice and for cause, terminate a contract at any time. The government remains liable for all costs the Company incurred in performing the contract through the date of termination. All proprietary knowledge gained, even if the contract is terminated, is retained by the Company and can be used for future R & D contracts or production contracts. The Company believes that as the application of the Company's R & D, technology to commercial applications continues to broaden, the effect of a termination, completion, or non-renewal of a specific R & D contract on the Company's financial or operational condition lessens.
The Company includes inflation and changing price provisions in its contracts and the Company intends to continue to seek such provisions in future contracts.
Year T vs. Year T-1: - -------------- Total revenues in Year T were approximately $859,000 compared to $3,380,000 in Year T-1, a decrease of 74.5%.
In Year T, revenues from research and development contracts was approximately $702,000 or 81.7% of total revenues as compared to $1,990,000 or 58.9% of total revenues in Year T-1. Revenues from equipment sales in Year T were approximately $157,000 or 18.3% of total revenues compared to $1,390,000 or 41.1% of total revenues in Year T-1. The decrease in revenues in both categories in Year T was due to delays in U.S. [NAME] funding for development and equipment contracts.
Total cost of sales in Year T was approximately $826,000 or 96.1% of total revenues as compared to $2,876,000 or 85.1% of total revenues in Year T-1. Contract R & D cost of sales in Year T was approximately $713,000 or 101.6% of Contract R & D revenues compared to $1,796,000 or 90.2% in Year T-1. In both Year T and Year T-1 the high cost of sales percentages for Contract R & D sales are due to a contract on which revenues exactly matched the costs. Except for such contract, the cost of sales percentages for Contract R & D sales would be 83.1% and 88.8% for Year T and Year T-1, respectively.
General and Administrative expenses were approximately $896,000 in Year T compared to $860,000 in Year T-1. Interest expense in Year T amounted to approximately $126,000 in Year T versus $57,000 in Year T-1. This increase is due to the higher level of debt during Year T and corresponding interest payments (see "Year T Financing).
In Year T, the Company incurred $457,000, or 53% of total revenues, for research and development expenditures on commercial application of its [NAME] technology compared to $238,000 or 7% of total revenues in Year T-1.
The Company recorded a net loss for Year T of approximately $1,448,000 compared to a net loss for Year T-1 of approximately $648,000. The greater loss in Year T is due to the significantly lower level of revenues in Year T and to higher spending on research and development than in Year T-1.
Year T-1 vs Year T-2: - ------------- Total revenues in Year T-1 were approximately $3,380,000 compared to $4,445,000 in Year T-2, a decrease of 24.0%.
In Year T-1, revenues from research and development contracts was approximately $1,990,000 or 58.9% of total revenues as compared to $3,048,000 or 68.6% of total revenues in Year T-2. Revenues from equipment sales in Year T-1 were approximately $1,390,000 or 41.1% of total revenues compared to $1,397,000 or 31.4% of total revenues in Year T-2. The decrease in contract research and development revenues in Year T-1 versus Year T-2 is due primarily to a reduction in U.S. [NAME] funding for such efforts.
Total cost of sales in Year T-1 was approximately $2,876,000 or 85.1% of total revenues as compared to $3,383,000 or 76.1% of total revenues in Year T-2. Contract R & D cost of sales in Year T-1 was approximately $1,796,000 or 90.2% of Contract R & D revenues compared to $2,341,000 or 76.8% in Year T-2. The higher cost of sales ratio and resulting decreased gross margin in Year T-1 is because approximately 12.9% of Contract R & D revenues resulted from a development program on which cost was shared equally with a commercial customer and a lower volume of orders received from the U.S. [NAME] which resulted in an unfavorable absorption of fixed overhead costs.
General and Administrative expenses were approximately $860,000 in Year T-1 compared to $850,000 in Year T-2. Interest expense in Year T-1 amounted to approximately $53,000 in Year T-1 versus approximately $61,000 in Year T-2.
In Year T-1, the Company incurred $238,000, or 7.0% of total revenues, for research and development in the area of optimizing spectrum efficiency for wireless communications related to the Company's expanded initiatives in commercial wireless communications. Any such expenses incurred in Year T-2 were minimal.
There was a net loss for Year T-1 of approximately $648,000 compared to net income in Year T-2 of approximately $152,000. The loss in Year T-1 is due to the lower revenue volume in Year T-1 for Contract R & D and increased expenses for research and development related to commercial wireless communications.
Year T-2 vs Year T-3: - ------------- Total revenues in Year T-2 were approximately $4,445,000 compared to $2,909,000 in Year T-3, an increase of 52.8%.
In Year T-2, revenues from research and development were approximately $3,048,000 or 68.6% of total revenues compared to $2,845,000 in Year T-3, or 97.8% of total revenues. Equipment revenues in Year T-2 were $1,397,000 or 31.4% of total revenues compared to $64,000 or 2.2% of total revenues in Year T-3. The increase in equipment revenues in Year T-2 is due mainly to revenues from a contract from the U.S. [NAME] for production of data terminal sets; whereas in Year T-3 equipment sales consisted of [NAME] computer boards to [NAME] Test Systems Division.
Total cost of sales in Year T-2 was approximately $3,383,000 or 76.1% of revenues versus $2,054,000 or 70.6% of revenue in Year T-3. R&D cost of sales in Year T-2 was approximately $2,341,000 76.8% of R&D revenue compared to $2,031,000 or 71.4% in Year T-3. Cost of sales for equipment was $1,041,000 or 74.5% of equipment revenue in Year T-2 compared to $23,000 or 35.9% in Year T-3.
The decrease in the total gross margin and for equipment sales in Year T-2 is due to the higher costs of the data terminal sets sold in Year T-2 which comprised 100% of Equipment Sales as compared to the [NAME] computer boards sold in Year T-3 which comprised 100% of Equipment Sales.
General and administrative expenses were approximately $850,000 in Year T-2 or 19.1% of revenue versus $679,000 or 23.3% of revenue. Interest expense in Year T-2 was approximately $61,000 or 1.4% of revenue compared to $25,000 in Year T-3 or 0.8% of revenue. Although total debt outstanding during Year T-2 was less than in Year T-3, a debt restructuring in Year T-3 called for a reduction in interest actually owed and paid.
Net Income in Year T-2 was $151,635 or 3.4% of revenue versus $447,140 or 15.4% of revenue in Year T-3. The difference is because Year T-3 included a gain of $295,893 from a restructuring of debt. Without such gain, Net Income in Year T-3 would have been $151,245.
Liquidity and Capital Resources - ------------------------------- Since its inception, the Company has financed its operations through debt, private and public offerings of equity securities and cash generated by operations.
In Year T, the Company had negative cash flow from operations of approximately $1,089,000 compared to negative cash flow from operations of $55,000 in Year T-1 and positive cash flow of $214,000 in Year T-2. There was negative working capital of $125,658 at December 31, Year T, negative working capital of $321,000 in Year T-1 and working capital of $287,000 at December 31, Year T-2.
A substantial portion of the Company's costs and expenses is represented by labor and related benefits. Such costs are recovered against R & D contracts with the government. They are paid by the Company monthly prior to the submission of invoices to the Federal government. Historically, any delays have not been significant and have not had a detrimental effect on the Company's operations. The Company believes that this will continue to be the case. In Year T, the Company increased its number of employees from 19 to 24.
The Company is dependent on Department of Defense contracts and could be vulnerable to future budget cuts by the U.S. [NAME] Department. The Company's present contracts are for equipment related to ongoing shipbuilding programs and the Company has no knowledge of budget cuts that would affect its present contracts. There can be no assurance, however, that such programs will not be subject to budget cuts.
