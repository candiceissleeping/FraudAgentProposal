                                                Summary

      1.      These proceedings arise out of an accounting and disclosure fraud case concerning
DMK Pharmaceuticals Corporation f/k/a Adamis Pharmaceuticals Corporation (“DMK
Pharmaceuticals” or “Adamis”) and Robert O. Hopkins, its former Chief Financial Officer.

         2.     From 2016 through 2021, Adamis and Hopkins generated a material percentage of
the company’s revenues through illegal prescriptions written by a veterinarian who was
compensated under a sham consulting agreement, and falsely recorded payments to the veterinarian
as legitimate consulting expenses. Adamis’s periodic reports filed with the Commission from 2016
through 2021 included improperly recognized revenue and had materially false and misleading
statements concerning risk factors. Hopkins was responsible for the company’s financial reporting
and periodic reports, and knew that the periodic reports were materially misleading because of the
sham agreement and payments to the veterinarian.

                                          Respondents

        3.      Respondent Adamis is a Delaware corporation with its principal executive offices
in San Diego, California. Its securities are registered pursuant to Section 12(g) of the Exchange
Act, formerly traded on the Nasdaq Capital Market, and currently trade on the Over-the-Counter
market Pink Limited Information tier operated by the OTC Markets Group under the symbol
DMKPQ. It filed periodic reports with the Commission during the relevant time period. It has no
disciplinary history.

        4.     The conduct at issue occurred in Adamis’s wholly-owned subsidiary, US
Compounding Inc., which during the relevant time was an Arkansas corporation with its principal
place of business in Conway, Arkansas. US Compounding ceased doing business at the end of
October 2021.

        5.       On May 25, 2023, Adamis merged with DMK Pharmaceuticals, a privately held
company, and the name of the surviving publicly traded Delaware corporation was changed to
DMK Pharmaceuticals Corporation. On February 2, 2024, DMK Pharmaceuticals and its
subsidiaries filed for bankruptcy under Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code in the United
States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware.

       6.      Respondent Robert O. Hopkins, age 64, resides in San Diego, California. He was
Vice President of Finance, Chief Financial Officer and Secretary of Adamis from 2009 until he left
in August 2021. He has an undergraduate degree in finance and a Master of Business
Administration. He has never been a Certified Public Accountant and has no disciplinary history.

                                              Facts

        7.     Adamis was a specialty biopharmaceutical company focused on developing and
commercializing products in various therapeutic areas, including allergy, opioid overdose,
respiratory and inflammatory disease.

        8.     Adamis’s wholly-owned subsidiary, US Compounding, was a registered drug
compounding outsourcing facility under the U.S. Food, Drug & Cosmetic Act. US Compounding
provided prescription compounded medications to patients, physician clinics, hospitals, surgery
centers and other clients throughout most of the United States, including veterinary pharmaceutical
products. As of July 30, 2021, Adamis began to wind down US Compounding’s business, and
effective October 31, 2021, US Compounding surrendered its Arkansas retail pharmacy permit and
wholesaler/outsourcer permit and was no longer selling compounded pharmaceutical or veterinary
products.

       9.      Hopkins joined a predecessor to Adamis in 2007 and continued working for
Adamis in San Diego until his departure in August 2021. As Adamis’s Vice President of Finance,
Chief Financial Officer and Secretary, Hopkins’s responsibilities included running the US
Compounding subsidiary as well as financial reporting by Adamis and its US Compounding

                                                 3
subsidiary. Hopkins was responsible for the company’s financial reporting and periodic reports.
Hopkins signed (i) management representation letters to the auditor, (ii) Adamis’s periodic reports
as the Principal Financial Officer and the Principal Accounting Officer, and (iii) the certifications
pursuant to Sections 302 and 906 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 (“SOX”).

        10.     Adamis filed with the Commission annual reports on Forms 10-K for the six
fiscal years ended December 31, 2016 through December 31, 2021, and quarterly reports on
Forms 10-Q for the 17 interim quarters beginning June 2016 that included improperly recognized
revenue and had materially false and misleading statements concerning risk factors.

                            Reporting of Revenues from Illegal Conduct

       11.      Adamis generated revenues from illegal conduct. During the period beginning with
Adamis’s acquisition of US Compounding in April 2016 until US Compounding stopped doing
business at the end of October 2021, the primary source of Adamis’s revenue was its US
Compounding subsidiary. From 2016 through 2018, US Compounding generated all of Adamis’s
revenue, and from 2019 through the first quarter of 2021 US Compounding generated over 80% of
Adamis’s revenue.

        12.      From 2016 through the first quarter of 2021, US Compounding sales
representatives improperly submitted some orders for animal medications that listed a veterinarian
as the prescribing doctor when he was not the actual veterinarian for the equine patients. Hopkins,
on behalf of Adamis, agreed to enter a “consulting agreement” with the veterinarian that falsely
stated that the veterinarian would be paid monthly based on an hourly consulting rate of $100,
while in fact the veterinarian’s monthly fee was based on an oral side agreement that he would be
paid a 10% commission on the illegal prescriptions. Adamis paid the veterinarian kickbacks
disguised as “consulting payments” that were actually a percentage of the sales on which he was
identified as the veterinarian.

         13.   Revenues relating to this illegal conduct were improperly recognized. The impact
of the fraud was material to Adamis’s reporting of revenues in its annual reports filed on
Forms 10-K and in its quarterly reports filed on Forms 10-Q.

         14.     In addition, the fraudulent revenues from prescriptions were also material because
they involved (i) unlawful acts conducted and concealed with the knowledge of the CFO,
(ii) intentional misstatements to the financial statements, and (iii) intentional false and misleading
statements by the CFO.

                                Disclosures about Two Risk Factors

       15.      In its annual reports on Forms 10-K, Adamis’s disclosures of two items as
hypothetical “risks” were materially misleading because the “risks” were things that had already
happened.


                                                   4
        16.     First, the annual reports for 2016 through 2020 disclosed risk about effective sales
infrastructure. When Adamis filed its annual reports for years 2016 through 2020, its CFO and
certain of US Compounding’s sales representatives were already engaged in the illegal conduct
described above. Therefore, noncompliant sales representatives were not a theoretical risk, but the
way of doing business for sales of certain equine prescription medication through one important
veterinarian.

         17.     Second, the annual reports for 2016 through 2021 disclosed risk about
noncompliance with federal and state statutes and regulations associated with its drug
compounding business. Noncompliance with federal laws was not a risk, but a reality. The sales
of prescription medications to equine patients using the veterinarian’s license when he did not have
a doctor-patient relationship violated the Food, Drug & Cosmetic Act. Furthermore, when he
prescribed medications in states where he did not have a license to practice veterinary medicine,
this also violated the law.

              Management Representation Letters and SOX Certifications by Hopkins

        18.     From 2016 through 2020, Hopkins signed management representation letters to the
auditor and certifications pursuant to SOX Sections 302 and 906 for both the annual reports on
Forms 10-K and the quarterly reports on Forms 10-Q. Contrary to Hopkins’s representations, he
knew that the filings (i) contained inaccurate financial statements with improperly recognized
revenue, and (ii) contained materially false and misleading statements.

                                 Securities Registration Statements

        19.      Between the time Adamis acquired US Compounding in April 2016 and the time
US Compounding ceased doing business in October 2021, Adamis offered and sold securities
pursuant to eight securities registration statements. Each of the periodic reports were incorporated
into the registration statements. Therefore, the improperly recognized revenue and false and
misleading statements concerning risk factors in the periodic reports discussed above were also
made in connection with these securities offerings.

                                    Adamis’s Remedial Efforts

        20.      In determining whether to accept the Offer, the Commission considered remedial
acts promptly undertaken by Adamis and cooperation afforded the Commission staff, including but
not limited to, the swift termination of all employees involved in the misconduct, the asset sale of
US Compounding, and the implementation of new leadership under DMK Pharmaceuticals who
had no involvement in the conduct at issue.

                                  Hopkins’s Cooperation Efforts

       21.     In determining whether to accept the Offer, the Commission considered
cooperation afforded the Commission staff by Hopkins, including but not limited to, appearing for

                                              Violations

        22.      As a result of the conduct described above, Adamis and Hopkins violated
Sections 17(a)(1) and (3) of the Securities Act, and Adamis violated, and Hopkins caused the
violation of, Section 17(a)(2) of the Securities Act, which prohibit any person from, in the offer or
sale of a security, employing “any device, scheme, or artifice to defraud” or engaging in any
“transaction, practice, or course of business” which operates or would operate as a fraud or deceit
upon the purchaser, or obtaining money or property by means of any materially false or misleading
statements.

        23.     As a result of the conduct described above, Adamis and Hopkins violated
Section 10(b) of the Exchange Act and Rule 10b-5 thereunder, which prohibit any person from, in
connection with the purchase or sale of a security, employing “any device, scheme, or artifice to
defraud” or engaging in any “transaction, practice, or course of business” which operates or would
operate as a fraud or deceit upon any person, or the making of any untrue statement of material fact
or the omission of a material fact necessary in order to make the statements made not misleading.

        24.     As a result of the conduct described above, Adamis violated, and Hopkins caused
the violations of, Section 13(a) of the Exchange Act and Rules 12b-20, 13a-1 and 13a-13
thereunder, which require issuers with a class of securities registered pursuant to Section 12 to file
accurate and complete annual and quarterly reports with the Commission and mandate that
periodic reports contain such further material information as may be necessary to make the
required statements not misleading.

         25.    As a result of the conduct described above, Adamis violated, and Hopkins caused
the violations of, Section 13(b)(2)(A) of the Exchange Act, which requires issuers with securities
registered pursuant to Section 12 to “make and keep books, records, and accounts, which, in
reasonable detail, accurately and fairly reflect the transactions and dispositions of the assets of the
issuer.”

        26.     As a result of the conduct described above, Hopkins willfully violated Exchange
Act Rule 13b2-1, which prohibits any person from, directly or indirectly, falsifying or causing to
be falsified any book, record, or account subject to Section 13(b)(2)(A) of the Exchange Act.

        27.     As a result of the conduct described above, Adamis violated, and Hopkins caused
the violations of, Section 13(b)(2)(B) of the Exchange Act, which, among other things, requires
issuers to devise and maintain a system of internal accounting controls sufficient to provide
reasonable assurances that transactions are recorded as necessary to permit preparation of financial
statements in conformity with GAAP.

       28.     As a result of the conduct described above, Hopkins willfully violated
Section 13(b)(5) of the Exchange Act by circumventing internal accounting controls concerning
the vendor payment processes by approving payments to the veterinarian that he knew were
improper.

        29.    As a result of the conduct described above, Hopkins willfully violated Exchange
Act Rule 13b2-2, which, among other things, prohibits an officer from making a materially false or
misleading statement to an accountant in connection with any required audit, review or
examination of the issuer’s financial statements or the preparation of a report required to be filed
with the Commission.

        30.     As a result of the conduct described above, Hopkins willfully violated Exchange
Act Rule 13a-14, which requires financial officers, among other things, to certify that the periodic
reports filed on Forms 10-K and 10-Q do not contain any untrue statement of a material fact or
omit to state a material fact necessary in order to make the statements made, in light of the
circumstances under which such statements were made, not misleading.

                                                 IV.
        In view of the foregoing, the Commission deems it appropriate to impose the sanctions
agreed to in Respondents’ Offers.

       Accordingly, it is hereby ORDERED that:

        A.      Pursuant to Section 8A of the Securities Act and Section 21C of the Exchange Act,
Respondent Adamis shall cease and desist from committing or causing any violations and any
future violations of Section 17(a) of the Securities Act and Sections 10(b), 13(a), 13(b)(2)(A) and
13(b)(2)(B) of the Exchange Act and Rules 10b-5, 12b-20, 13a-1, and 13a-13 thereunder.

       B.       Pursuant to Section 8A of the Securities Act and Section 21C of the Exchange Act,
Respondent Adamis is ordered to pay disgorgement $313,613 and prejudgment interest of
$20,701, for a total payment of $334,314, which amount is deemed satisfied by the forfeiture
payments ordered in a parallel criminal action.

        C.      Pursuant to Section 8A of the Securities Act and Section 21C of the Exchange Act,
Respondent Hopkins shall cease and desist from committing or causing any violations and any
future violations of Section 17(a) of the Securities Act and Sections 10(b), 13(a), 13(b)(2)(A),
13(b)(2)(B) and 13(b)(5) of the Exchange Act and Rules 10b-5, 12b-20, 13a-1, 13a-13, Rule 13a-
14, 13b2-1 and 13b2-2 thereunder.

        D.       Pursuant to Section 8A of the Securities Act and Sections 4C of the Exchange Act,
Respondent Hopkins is permanently barred from acting as an officer or director of any issuer that
has a class of securities registered pursuant to Section 12 of the Exchange Act or that is required to
file reports pursuant to Section 15(d) of the Exchange.
